# Why Your Sales Pipeline Is Inconsistent and How to Fix It

The pattern is so common it has a rhythm: a great quarter, everyone too busy delivering to sell, then the cliff, then the scramble, then a great quarter born of panic. Founders blame the market, the team, or the season. The actual cause is structural, and it has five known failure points.

## Cause 1: Selling only happens when delivery is slow

When the founder is the pipeline, prospecting is the first thing sacrificed to delivery. The famine you feel in March was created by the busy season in November. The fix is not discipline, founders are plenty disciplined, it is removing prospecting from the list of things that compete with delivery at all. Demand generation has to run on infrastructure, not on willpower.

## Cause 2: One channel, and it is referrals

Referrals are wonderful and uncontrollable. You cannot turn the dial up in a slow month. A pipeline needs at least one channel you control completely, outbound is the purest example, and one that compounds without attention, which is what content and search are for. Referrals then become upside instead of the plan.

## Cause 3: Leads die in the seam

Marketing produces inquiries, sales is busy, and the industry's standing disgrace is that over 70 percent of marketing leads are never contacted. Five plus follow ups are needed to convert a typical B2B prospect, and almost nobody's internal process survives follow up number two. The seam between attention and conversation is where pipelines actually die, and it is a process problem, which means it is fixable by process.

## Cause 4: The math has never been written down

Most companies cannot answer: how many meetings produce one client, how many conversations produce one meeting, how much demand produces one conversation. Without that chain, every month is a surprise. With it, pipeline becomes arithmetic: revenue target, divided by close rate, equals meetings needed, equals a demand number someone can be accountable for.

## Cause 5: Four vendors, zero owners

A web shop, an ads vendor, maybe an SDR firm, none of whom have met. Each optimizes its own metric and the pipeline falls between them. Accountability for the whole chain has to live somewhere, in a hire, in a firm, somewhere with a number attached.

## The fix, in order

Write the math. Stand up one controlled channel and one compounding channel. Build the follow up machine so no inquiry dies of silence. Then put one name on the whole number. Companies that do the four steps stop having quarters that feel like weather.
