# How to Get More B2B Sales Meetings Without Hiring an SDR

The question every founder eventually types into a search bar. Here is the operator's answer: five mechanisms, the numbers behind each, the step in our system each one belongs to, and the order that makes them compound instead of compete.

## 1. Fix the seam before adding volume

Before any new demand, audit what already arrives. More than 70 percent of marketing leads are never contacted by sales at all. A PI who requests a quote on a Friday and hears nothing until Wednesday has already emailed your competitor. Inquiries answered within five minutes convert at multiples of inquiries answered the next day, and the typical B2B prospect needs five or more touches before committing to a meeting. A response time standard plus a written nurture sequence is the cheapest meeting volume you will ever buy, because the leads are already paid for. In our system this is the lead handoff runbook, and it ships on Catalyst as follow up within one business day.

## 2. A calendar path with no friction

Audit your own site as a stranger: how many clicks from arrival to a bookable meeting? Every form field, approval step, and back and forth email subtracts meetings. The pattern that books: a visible calendar, a single qualifying question, and pricing published so the prospect arrives ready rather than fishing. This is step one, Build. A practice site that makes a patient call to ask the price loses the patient to the practice that publishes it.

## 3. Content that answers the meeting before it happens

The pages that create meetings are not thought leadership. They are the diligence pages: pricing, comparisons, how it works, proof with numbers, and for a scientific buyer, the protocol, the validation data, and the model comparison. BioInformatics, which surveys life scientists on buying behavior, reports that customers complete 60 percent or more of the sales process before contacting a rep. A prospect who has read those pages arrives at the meeting half closed. This is step two, Rank, and it is why Baseline at $2,500 exists as a starting point: publish the answers your sales conversations repeat, and the meetings both multiply and improve.

## 4. Outbound, sized honestly

Cold outreach works when it is researched, multichannel, and persistent, and produces noise when it is a purchased list and a mail merge. Well built lists and real personalization produce reply rates in the low to mid single digits, and it takes coordinated email, phone, and LinkedIn touches over weeks to convert replies into held meetings. In technical markets the list matters more than the copy: for one healthcare revenue client we built the prospect universe from federal public use files before a single email went out. Sized correctly, a serious program reaches a few hundred right accounts deeply rather than ten thousand badly. This is step five, Book, one seat on Kinetic with a meeting number in writing.

## 5. Familiarity, bought deliberately

Reply rates and show rates track recognition. Founder content on LinkedIn, a consistent drumbeat in the channels your buyers scroll, and for businesses ready for it, streaming TV in target geographies, all raise the temperature of every cold touch. Steps three and four, Run and Buy. This is the mechanism that makes the other four cheaper, which is why it runs continuously rather than as a campaign.

## The sequencing

Seam first, calendar path second, diligence content third, outbound fourth, familiarity always. Run in that order and each mechanism feeds the next. Teams that want to run it themselves buy step six, Educate: the Protocol, one or two days at your office with the runbooks in your brand.
