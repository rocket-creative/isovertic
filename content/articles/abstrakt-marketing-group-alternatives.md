# 5 Abstrakt Marketing Group Alternatives for 2026

[Abstrakt Marketing Group](/compare/abstrakt-marketing-group-alternative) earned its position: 500 plus employees, more than 1,700 clients, a long Inc. 5000 streak, and a real model combining outbound SDR teams with marketing services, plus industry exclusivity by territory. If you are researching alternatives, it is usually for one of three reasons. You sell something a generalist caller cannot explain, a mouse model, a diagnostic, a surgical program. You market under rules a volume shop does not know, HIPAA or medical review. Or you want a price you can see before a sales process. Here are five alternatives, sorted by which reason is yours.

1. ISOVERTIC. For scientists, startups, and healthcare brands that need the whole pipeline from a team that reads the paper before it writes the email. Six steps, Build, Rank, Run, Buy, Book, Educate, sold as four published tiers from $2,500 a month for content to $10,000 for the full pipeline with an outbound seat and a meeting number in writing. The [MLR review gate](/pricing) and the HIPAA form stack are add ons, not afterthoughts, and the COO spent a decade selling for a genetic engineering CRO. The honest tradeoff: a short roster by design, so we are the opposite of a volume vendor.
2. [Belkins](/compare/belkins-alternative). For companies whose only gap is outbound and whose product a smart generalist can pitch. A respected pure play [appointment setting](/outbound-appointment-setting) firm with strong process and review presence. No marketing services, which is the point.
3. CIENCE. For data heavy outbound at scale. Large list building and research operation paired with SDR teams. Ask to meet your assigned pod before signing; consistency tracks the team you get.
4. Callbox. For global and volume programs. Long operating history, multiregion coverage, published case numbers. The most machine like of the pure plays.
5. Martal Group. For North American tech and B2B services wanting senior fractional sellers rather than junior SDR benches.

## Where Abstrakt fits and where it does not

Abstrakt suits trades and SMBs with a simple offer and a large addressable market: HVAC, commercial cleaning, staffing. Its exclusivity model rewards that. It is a poor fit when the buyer is a principal investigator who will test every claim against the literature, or a practice whose intake form is protected health information. Those buyers punish generic outreach and reward a [website](/web-development) that answers the technical question before the call.

## How to run the evaluation

Whatever list you end with, force three artifacts before signature: the names and tenure of your actual team, the day 90 number in writing, and the ownership terms for lists, copy, accounts, and code. Add a fourth if you are regulated: who signs off on a clinical claim before it publishes, and where that sign off is logged. Any firm above can be a right answer. The artifacts tell you which one is right for you.
