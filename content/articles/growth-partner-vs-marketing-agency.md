# Growth Partner vs Marketing Agency: What You Are Actually Buying

A confession from inside the industry: the traditional agency model is designed so the agency gets paid whether or not you grow. The fee bills on the first of the month. The report celebrates impressions. And everything that happens after the click, your CRM, your sales conversations, your close rate, is treated as your problem. In B2B and high ticket business, most of the sale happens after the click, which means the traditional agency has structured itself out of accountability for most of the sale.

## The structural difference

An agency sells activities: posts published, campaigns managed, hours worked. A growth partner sells a system with an outcome attached: pipeline created, meetings booked, revenue influenced, with compensation and reporting built around those numbers. The difference shows up in three places. Scope: agencies stop at the click, growth partners run through the calendar. Metrics: activity versus business impact. Incentives: a fixed fee with no number attached versus a commitment someone has to re earn.

## The tell in the first meeting

Ask one question: what number will you put in writing for day 90? An agency will talk about how every business is different. A growth partner will ask about your deal size, close rate, and capacity, then give you a number and the mechanism behind it. The willingness to be measured is the entire difference, and you can detect it in under five minutes.

## When an agency is actually the right call

Fairness requires saying it: if you need a brand campaign, a launch, or a specific creative project, a traditional agency is built for exactly that, and a growth partner is overkill. The partner model fits when the problem is the pipeline itself: revenue is inconsistent, vendors do not talk to each other, and nobody owns the number.

## The pricing logic

Partners cost more per month and less per outcome. The in house alternative, one marketing lead plus one agency retainer, runs $160K to $190K a year with the seam between them still unowned. A pipeline partner prices below that stack because one team shares the work, and the better ones put a meeting number in writing so the cost per outcome is knowable before you sign. Ours are published on the pricing page, which is itself a tell about which model a firm runs.
