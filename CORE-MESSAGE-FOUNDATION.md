# Core Message Foundation

Status: the positioning canon. Nothing here ships verbatim, but every line of site copy that says what ISOVERTIC is traces back to this page. Written September 8, 2026, from the George and Kristen call of the same day. Supersedes the September 8 morning draft, which led with the website.

## The one sentence

The growth agency for companies that sell to specialists. One senior team owns your pipeline from the first search to the booked meeting.

That is the hat. It is what the homepage H1 says, what the meta title says, and what a client repeats when someone asks who we are.

## What we are and what we are not

We are a growth agency. Not a marketing agency (they hand you traffic and reports), not a web design company (they hand you a launch), not an appointment setter (they book meetings into whatever you already have). We run the whole pipeline inside one boundary, on one method, with one number at the end: booked meetings. The client closes.

Say "growth agency" or "ISOVERTIC." Do not say growth firm, growth company, growth engine, capture engine, pipeline systems shop, pipeline shop, full service agency, or firm as the noun for the company. "Firm" survives only in generic phrases about other firms.

## The promise

We turn the specialist already searching for what you sell into a booked meeting, and we can prove which page and which campaign produced it.

The word is meetings. Not leads (the client still has to close them), not customers (that is the client's job, and the site says so). A meeting is the thing we can be judged on, and Amplification and above carry the number in writing.

## The method is the how, not the hat

The long tail website, the keyword architecture, the site architecture, the specialist page templates, the server side tracking, the compliance wiring, the drift monitoring, the catalog expansion: that is the method under step one. It is the reason the promise holds and the reason a generalist cannot copy the result. It is also the part we keep in house.

Rules for it on the site:

- It appears once per page at most, after the promise and after the proof, never in a hero or a meta title.
- Call it "the method" or "the same method on every account." Do not name it "the seven layer method" outside /system, where the seven layers are listed. Kristen's test: a CEO of a mouse model company should understand the sentence without a glossary.
- Never describe it as the product. The product is the pipeline and the meeting.

## Why one team

Most growing companies buy the pipeline in pieces: a web guy, an ads guy, an SEO vendor, a rented sales team. Every vendor hits its own number and nobody owns the meeting. The scattered team is the problem we replace. George saw the whole pipeline as one system and engineered the method; Kristen wrote it down, trained the team on it, and built the operation that runs it the same way on every account. The founder is the source and the escalation path, not the bottleneck.

## Who this is for

Companies that sell to specialists: scientists, clinicians, billing directors, engineers, procurement offices. Specialists search in exact vocabulary and almost nobody competes for those terms, so the demand exists and is unclaimed.

Two buyers say yes:

1. Ready to grow, no experienced team. Usually scientists who spun out of academia, or a founder with a raise to spend. They would rather hire one team than five specialists.
2. Leads dried up. Something changed and the pipeline that used to work does not. They need help now.

The wrong buyer: overwhelmed, making enough to survive, and not interested in more responsibility. Not our job to convince anyone it is time to grow. Where growth would drown the owner, the pitch is relief first: go inside the company, map how a lead moves through it, find where it leaks, automate what does not need a person, write the SOPs, then add volume. On the site and in the agreement this is a deliverable (the map, the automations we build, the SOPs), never an outcome and never a promise that the business or its profit will change; plug the leaks and fix the business stay in the sales conversation. That work is in scope on Quantum Leap (the operations audit line in the tier), not a separate product.

## Proof before mechanism

Lead with numbers, then explain. ITL: roughly 5x monthly form fills and 3.7x organic clicks, months five through eight against the prior six month baseline. Sydra: a company that did not exist online before mid June 2026, now about one qualified lead a week on roughly $1,000 a month in ads.

Site rule still applies: case studies show multiples and percentages only, first eight months, no raw counts, and a client's numbers appear beside its name only after written permission.

## Two honest limits

1. This works when buyers already search with specific terms. If nobody searches for your category yet, this method will not find them, and we will say so before you pay us.
2. A lead a week on a small budget is a trickle, not a flood. It is the right result for the spend.

- /system: the six steps, then the method under step one with the seven layers listed. The only page that counts the layers.
- /about: what you are buying (company knowledge, one team), who is on it, George and Kristen.
- /results: numbers first, mechanism second, per the case study rule.
- Industry and service pages: the industry's own vocabulary, the tier to start on, and the meeting as the end result. "Growth agency" replaces "marketing agency" in H1s and titles; "marketing" stays as a word so the page still matches what people type.
- Field notes: written for the client, not for other marketers.
- Sales calls and proposals: open with the ITL numbers before any explanation.
