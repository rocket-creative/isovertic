# Core Message Foundation

Status: the positioning canon. Nothing here ships verbatim, but every line of site copy that says what ISOVERTIC is traces back to this page. Updated September 10, 2026 from the Site Rewrite v3. Supersedes the September 8 draft.

## The one sentence

The growth agency for healthcare, biotech, medical devices, and life sciences. One senior team owns your pipeline from the first search to the booked meeting.

That is the hat. It is what the homepage H1 says, what the meta title says, and what a client repeats when someone asks who we are.

## What we are and what we are not

We are a growth agency. Not a marketing agency (they hand you traffic and reports), not a web design company (they hand you a launch), not an appointment setter (they book meetings into whatever you already have). We run the whole pipeline inside one boundary, on one method, with one number at the end: booked meetings. The client closes.

Say "growth agency" or "ISOVERTIC." Do not say growth firm, growth company, growth engine, capture engine, pipeline systems shop, pipeline shop, full service agency, or firm as the noun for the company. "Firm" survives only in generic phrases about other firms.

## The promise

We build the website that gets found by the specialist already searching for what you sell, then we spend advertising only on those people.

The word is meetings. Not leads (the client still has to close them), not customers (that is the client's job, and the site says so). A meeting is the thing we can be judged on, and Amplification and above carry the number in writing.

## The method is the how, not the hat

The long tail website, the keyword architecture, the site architecture, the specialist page templates, the server side tracking, the compliance wiring, the drift monitoring, the catalog expansion: that is the method under step one. It is the reason the promise holds. It is also the work we sell, and we do not publish it.

Rules for it on the site:

- Call it "the method" or "the same method on every account." Do not name it "the seven layer method" on any public page. Do not list the seven layers. Do not describe how we choose what to publish, bid on, or build.
- Never describe it as the product. The product is the pipeline and the meeting.
- Say we have a method and what it produces. Not how.

## Four industries, named on every page

Every public page names all four served industries at least once:

1. Healthcare: practice groups, hospitals, health systems, revenue-cycle companies, dental and vet and urgent-care groups, telehealth, and digital health.
2. Biotech: therapeutics companies pre-clinical through commercial, research services, translational programs, and platform companies with a scientific buyer.
3. Medical devices: 510(k) and PMA companies making implants, diagnostics, surgical instruments, capital equipment, in vitro diagnostics, and wearables.
4. Life sciences tools and diagnostics: reagents, antibodies, kits, instruments, mouse models, cell lines, laboratory automation, and diagnostics companies that sell into hospitals and reference labs.

Startups, professional services, home services, and hospitality remain as industry pages, but they are not the category label.

Do not use "technical B2B" as an industry. Buyers do not call themselves that.

## Why one team

Most growing companies buy the pipeline in pieces: a web vendor, an ads vendor, an SEO vendor, a rented sales team. Every vendor hits its own number and nobody owns the meeting. The scattered team is the problem we replace. George saw the whole pipeline as one system and engineered the method; Kristen wrote it down, trained the team on it, and built the operation that runs it the same way on every account. The founder is the source and the escalation path, not the bottleneck.

## Who this is for

Write for the reader in their title: founders, CEOs, presidents, VPs of commercial, chief scientific officers, chief medical officers, medical directors, principal investigators, VPs of regulatory, practice administrators, directors of billing.

Two buyers say yes:

1. Ready to grow, no experienced team. Usually scientists who spun out of academia, or a founder with a raise to spend. They would rather hire one team than five specialists.
2. Leads dried up. Something changed and the pipeline that used to work does not. They need help now.

The wrong buyer: overwhelmed, making enough to survive, and not interested in more responsibility. Not our job to convince anyone it is time to grow. Where growth would drown the owner, the pitch is relief first. That work is in scope on Quantum Leap (the operations audit line in the tier), not a separate product.

## Proof before mechanism

Lead with numbers, then explain. Ingenious Targeting Laboratory: monthly form submissions moved from 13 to about 64. Organic search traffic rose 3.7 times. First eight months. Internal, September 2026. Sydra Health: a company that did not exist online before mid June 2026, now about one qualified meeting a week on roughly $1,000 a month in ads plus $2,500 in monthly site work.

Public case studies may name the client with those figures when written permission is on file, as it is for Ingenious Targeting Laboratory and Sydra Health. Both are unusual for their category and neither is a promise for yours.

## Two honest limits

1. This works when buyers already search with specific terms. If nobody searches for your category yet, this method will not find them, and we will say so before you pay us.
2. A qualified meeting a week on a small budget is a trickle, not a flood. It is the right result for the spend.

## Vocabulary

| Say | Do not say |
|---|---|
| growth agency | growth firm, growth company, growth engine, capture engine, pipeline shop, full service agency |
| booked meeting, meeting on your calendar | customers we deliver, leads as the end result |
| the method, the same method on every account | the seven layer method, long tail, hub and spoke, keyword architecture |
| one senior team owns the pipeline | we build the website (as the lead claim) |
| healthcare, biotech, medical devices, and life sciences | companies that sell to specialists (as the only category label) |
| Ground State, Excitation, Amplification, Quantum Leap | Baseline, Catalyst, Kinetic, Critical Mass |
| six steps: Build, Rank, Run, Buy, Book, Educate; you close | seven steps, funnel, growth hacking |
| a published article, a newsletter, eight social posts, a landing page, a monthly report | strategy, optimization, content flywheel |

Also banned in public copy: creating demand, brand awareness, funnel, growth hacking, ROI framework, Next.js, server-rendered, schema, hub and spoke, SERP, MQL, SQL, ICP. They describe another playbook or they describe the method.

Describe deliverables in words the reader would use in a status meeting: a monthly report, a published article, a newsletter, eight social posts, a landing page, a qualified meeting on the calendar with a written brief.

## Where each idea lives

- Homepage: hero is the category sentence. Then who we work with, then what we do, then proof, then the four packages, then the six steps.
- /system: the six steps, what shows up, and the honest limit for each. No layer list.
- /about: what you are buying (company knowledge, one team), who is on it, George and Kristen.
- /results: numbers first, mechanism second, per the case study rule.
- Industry and service pages: the industry's own vocabulary, the package to start on, and the meeting as the end result. "Growth agency" in H1s and titles; "marketing" stays as a word so the page still matches what people type.
- Field notes: written for the client, not for other marketers.

## Dual CTA

Every page ends with two next steps:

1. Start on the pricing page with card or bank debit. No call required.
2. Book a pipeline call, send us your best-converting landing page, and in about ten minutes we will tell you what is working. Sometimes the honest answer is you are already fine, and we are happy to say so and hand you back your afternoon.
