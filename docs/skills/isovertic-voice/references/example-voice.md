# Voice in Action: Three Annotated Excerpts

Three shipped Isovertic openings, with the moves called out. Copy the move, not the exact words.

**Note on this file:** These are direct excerpts from shipped pieces, quoted verbatim to preserve the exact voice. Running the audit on this file will produce hits (that's expected). The audit is meant to run on your drafts, not on the reference examples.

## Excerpt 1 — The HIPAA opening (warm, reassured, promises checklist)

> The good news first, because with HIPAA content that's a small kindness worth paying up front: none of this is as scary as it looks, and there's a clean stack that solves it. If you're a practice administrator, a medical director, or a healthcare marketing lead who's been getting nervous every time your team ships a new landing page, you're the right reader for this and you're going to leave with a checklist.
>
> Here's the honest part. In the last three years, tracking pixels on healthcare websites have produced hundreds of class-action lawsuits and at least three settlements north of $6 million. None of them involved a hack. Every one of them involved marketing tools installed the way marketing tools are usually installed: by well-meaning people who followed the vendor's setup instructions and had no reason to suspect they were installing a lawsuit.

**Moves used:**
1. `The good news first, because [reason] worth paying up front` — warm frame that acknowledges the topic is heavy
2. `If you're [specific role 1], [role 2], or [role 3] who's been [specific anxiety]` — direct address to three named reader personas with the exact anxiety
3. `you're the right reader for this and you're going to leave with [specific artifact]` — the promise, named
4. `Here's the honest part.` — the transition to the real stakes
5. `hundreds of class-action lawsuits and at least three settlements north of $6 million` — the concrete number, no hedge
6. `None of them involved a hack. Every one of them involved [thing they'd never suspect]` — the twist that makes the reader lean in

## Excerpt 2 — The MQL opening (name the fiction, promise the fix)

> Marketing produces a lead. Sales says the lead is unqualified. Marketing says the lead was qualified. Both look at the same spreadsheet and see different sports. This is not a data problem. It is a pipeline-ownership problem, and the industry gave it the wrong name — "the MQL." Once you rename the problem, the fix stops feeling political.

**Moves used:**
1. Four short sentences that any operator recognizes from their last Monday meeting
2. `Both look at the same spreadsheet and see different sports` — a small dry image, not a joke
3. `This is not a data problem. It is a [X] problem` — the reframe move
4. `Once you [do the thing], the [previously political thing] stops feeling political` — the promise of relief

## Excerpt 3 — The quarterly reviews opening (name the mismatch, invoke the reader's own experience)

> Every quarter, someone senior at your company looks at a marketing report and asks the same question: "Why aren't we seeing revenue from this yet?" If the sales cycle is six months and the campaign started in month two, the honest answer is that the campaign has not finished yet. But the honest answer sounds like an excuse, so nobody gives it, so the campaign gets cut, so the pipeline it was building never lands, so the review next quarter is worse, so the next campaign is cut faster.

**Moves used:**
1. `Every quarter, someone senior at your company looks at a marketing report and asks the same question` — the reader can hear the meeting
2. Direct quote in quotes — makes it visceral, not abstract
3. `the honest answer is that [thing]. But the honest answer sounds like an excuse` — names the political trap
4. The `so… so… so… so…` cascade — mimics the actual doom loop the reader is living

## What all three have in common

- **First person or direct address in the first two sentences.** "You're the right reader." "Every quarter, someone senior at your company." Never "In today's healthcare landscape."
- **A concrete, specific, sourced number in paragraph one or two.** No warm-up.
- **One small human observation** ("see different sports" / "small kindness worth paying up front" / the cascade). This is the trust move.
- **A specific artifact promised.** Checklist. Reframe. Two-clock scorecard. Never "insights" or "learnings."

## What none of them do

- No "Introduction" header
- No "In today's fast-paced world"
- No "Let's dive into"
- No em-dashes
- No hero image described in the copy
- No "5 reasons why"
- No question hooks ("Ever wonder why…?")
