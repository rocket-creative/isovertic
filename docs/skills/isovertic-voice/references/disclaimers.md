# House Disclaimers

Two disclaimers, ready to paste. Both are plain-language. Neither is legal advice, and both say so.

## HIPAA / healthcare disclaimer

Paste at the end of any piece that discusses HIPAA, OCR guidance, tracking pixels, BAAs, PHI, patient data, or healthcare marketing compliance.

```
---

*This piece is a marketing operating framework, not legal advice. HIPAA compliance depends on your specific facts, jurisdiction, and covered-entity or business-associate status. The controlling sources are 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164, OCR's [Revised Bulletin on tracking technologies](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html), and the June 20, 2024 opinion in American Hospital Association v. Becerra, No. 4:23-cv-01110-P (N.D. Tex.). Consult qualified healthcare counsel before implementing any of the above.*
```

## General legal / regulated-industry disclaimer

Paste at the end of any piece that discusses financial services, insurance, cannabis, gambling, alcohol, legal services, or FTC enforcement.

```
---

*This piece is a marketing operating framework, not legal advice. The rules in [category] change often and depend on jurisdiction, license status, and the specific facts of your situation. Consult qualified [category] counsel before implementing any of the above.*
```

## When NOT to include a disclaimer

- Pure marketing strategy pieces (positioning, funnel, measurement)
- Client case studies with no regulatory claim
- Landing pages and CTAs
- Sales emails
- LinkedIn posts

## When the disclaimer should be inline, not at the end

If a piece makes a specific legal claim that could be read as a rule ("you can now put a Meta pixel on a blog post"), the disclaimer for that specific claim goes in the same paragraph, not just at the end. Example:

> The June 2024 ruling makes a Meta pixel arguable on a public blog post about high blood pressure (arguable, not guaranteed; talk to counsel). It did not make it safe on a page where a patient can book an appointment.
