# Isovertic Marketing

## When to load

Any Isovertic marketing task involving strategy, positioning, planning, or client work:

- Isovertic's own positioning, ICP, offer, pricing, engagement length
- Client strategy, what playbook fits a given client, what to recommend, how to measure
- Campaign, funnel, or GTM design for an Isovertic client
- Briefs, proposals, scope documents, measurement plans
- Answering prospect objections (especially "why 12 months," "why is the website included," "why $750 a month for the HIPAA stack")

**Load `isovertic-voice` alongside this skill for any task that produces written output.** Both skills live in the site repo at `docs/skills/`; the reference files below are relative to `docs/skills/isovertic-marketing/`. They compose. This skill tells you what to say; `isovertic-voice` tells you how to say it.

Do not load this skill for pure marketing execution with no strategy angle (a blog draft, a LinkedIn post), `isovertic-voice` alone is enough.

## Step 1: Isovertic's positioning, in one page

Source of truth: `CORE-MESSAGE-FOUNDATION.md` at the repo root and the live site (isovertic.com). If this section and that file disagree, the file wins. Updated September 10, 2026.

**The one sentence.** The growth agency for healthcare, biotech, medical devices, and life sciences. One senior team owns your pipeline from the first search to the booked meeting.

**What Isovertic is.** A growth agency. Not a marketing agency (traffic and reports), not a web design company (a launch), not an appointment setter (meetings booked into whatever you already have). Six steps inside one boundary: Build, Rank, Run, Buy, Book, Educate. The client closes. The end result is a booked meeting, and Amplification and above carry the meeting number in writing.

**Vocabulary.** Say "growth agency" or "ISOVERTIC." Never growth firm, growth company, growth engine, capture engine, pipeline systems shop, pipeline shop, or full service agency. Say "meetings," not customers we deliver. Say "the method" or "the same method on every account." Do not name or list the seven layers on any public page. Do not describe how we choose what to publish, bid on, or build.

**ICP (ideal client):** healthcare, biotech, medical devices, and life sciences companies whose buyers are specialists (scientists, clinicians, billing directors, VPs of regulatory, practice administrators), because specialists search in exact vocabulary and almost nobody competes for those terms.
- Healthcare: practice groups, hospitals, health systems, revenue-cycle companies, dental and vet and urgent-care groups, telehealth, and digital health.
- Biotech: therapeutics companies pre-clinical through commercial, research services, translational programs, and platform companies with a scientific buyer.
- Medical devices: 510(k) and PMA companies making implants, diagnostics, surgical instruments, capital equipment, in vitro diagnostics, and wearables.
- Life sciences tools and diagnostics: reagents, antibodies, kits, instruments, mouse models, cell lines, laboratory automation, and diagnostics sold into hospitals and reference labs.
- Startups from pre revenue (Ground State) to Series A with a board asking for pipeline (Amplification).
- Professional services, home services, and hospitality each still have an industry page and a starting tier. Do not use "technical B2B" as an industry.

**Two buyers say yes.** (1) Ready to grow, no experienced team: usually scientists who spun out of academia, or a founder with a raise to spend, who would rather hire one team than five specialists. (2) Leads dried up: something changed and the pipeline that used to work does not.

**Anti-ICP (say no):**
- The overwhelmed owner who is making enough to survive and does not want more responsibility. It is not our job to convince anyone it is time to grow. Where growth would drown the owner, the honest pitch is relief first (fix the pipeline and the operation, then add volume), which is in scope on Quantum Leap as the operations audit (map how a lead moves through the company, find where it leaks, automate, write the SOPs, then add volume; a deliverable, not an outcome), not a separate product.
- Categories nobody searches for yet. This method finds buyers who already search with specific terms; category creation is a different playbook, and we say so before they pay.
- Clients demanding revenue judgment at 90 days on a long cycle (see `references/engagement-length-defense.md`).
- E-commerce and DTC.

**Offer shape (pricing source of truth is `content/tiers.ts`):**
- Ground State, $2,500 a month: the content drumbeat, running without meetings.
- Excitation, $5,000 plus ad spend: content plus paid demand, with a human on the account.
- Amplification, $10,000 plus ad spend: the full pipeline, one outbound seat, meeting number in writing. The flagship.
- Quantum Leap, from $25,000 plus media: per brand, two outbound seats, streaming and broadcast TV, and the operations audit (the map, the automations, the SOPs) before volume is added.
- Every tier is a 12 month term. No month to month. The $15,000 website rebuild and hosting are included in every term.
- Protocol: the client's team taught the system at their office, $5,000 one day, $20,000 full team, $3,500 as a client add on.
- Add-ons include the HIPAA form and call stack at $750 a month, the MLR review gate, extra outbound seats, programmatic page expansion, and a CRM build. Full list on /pricing.
- George is on every account as the standards owner and the escalation path. Kristen is on every life science account.

**Pricing frames we use:**
- "Roughly two hours of the healthcare litigation counsel who would defend the pixel lawsuit this stack prevents", for the HIPAA stack
- "Less than the fully loaded cost of one in house marketing hire, with zero ramp", for Amplification
- Published pricing. Never "custom" without a floor.

**Proof comes before mechanism.** Open with ITL (roughly 5x monthly form fills, 3.7x organic clicks, months five through eight against the prior six month baseline) and Sydra (a company that did not exist online before mid June 2026, about one qualified lead a week on roughly $1,000 a month in ads). Public case studies show multiples only, first eight months, and a client's numbers appear beside its name only with written permission.

**Two honest limits, said out loud.** This works when buyers already search with specific terms. A lead a week on a small budget is a trickle, not a flood, and it is the right result for the spend.

**The Isovertic move on discovery calls.**
Send us your best converting landing page URL. In about ten minutes we tell you what is firing, whether any of it is a lawsuit, and whether you are already fine. Sometimes the honest answer is you are fine and we hand you back your afternoon.

## Step 2: Defend the engagement length (the single most common objection)

Prospects will ask for 90-day pilots, monthly rolling contracts, or quarterly revenue judgments. All three are red flags. `references/engagement-length-defense.md` is the full research memo, read it before answering the objection.

The short version to hand a prospect:

- **Google's own guidance says SEO takes "four months to a year"** ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names)). Ahrefs' May 2025 study found only 1.74% of new pages reach the top 10 within a year.
- **Healthcare / biotech / medtech sales cycles are 125 days to 24 months.** A lead generated in month 2 may not close until month 12 to 20.
- **Median B2B SaaS CAC payback is 16 months** (2026 Aleph × Benchmarkit benchmarks). Enterprise (>$100K ACV) runs 18 to 24 months.
- **43% of B2B agency churn happens in the first 90 days**, before any work can show a result (Focus Digital 2026 churn report).
- **In-house marketing hires get 6 to 12 months before performance is judged.** CMO tenure averages 4.1 years. If they wouldn't fire a CMO after 90 days, they can't fire the growth agency after 90 days either.

**The two-clock scorecard.** Commit to leading-indicator reporting every 30 days (rankings, impressions, CTR, CPC, conversions, cost per lead, learning-phase status) and lagging-indicator reviews at 6 and 12 months (pipeline, revenue, CAC payback). Tie the lagging review window to the client's actual sales cycle. This is in every proposal.

**When to hold firm.** The site sells a 12 month term on every tier and nothing shorter, because the website build is included and content and brand work do not move in a quarter. When the sales cycle is under 90 days and the offer is paid search led, paid search shows a real cost per meeting inside the ramp and is reported at day 90; the term is still a year, and revenue on a short cycle is judged at month six. If a prospect demands revenue judgment at 90 days on a long cycle deal, treat it as a red flag and reset expectations before signing; that expectation, more than budget, predicts churn.

## Step 3: Match the client to a playbook

Isovertic works across models. Read the playbook that fits the client before recommending tactics. If two clearly apply (e.g., healthcare SaaS with a PLG motion), read both.

| `references/playbook-regulated-industries.md` | Any regulated category where compliance shapes every asset (financial services, insurance, cannabis, gambling, alcohol, legal) |

| `references/playbook-agencies-professional-services.md` | Agency, consultancy, law/accounting firm, or expert-services shop selling billable time or retainers |
| `references/playbook-franchise-multi-location.md` | Franchise systems or multi-location brands balancing HQ brand and local operator demand (e.g., multi-site dental groups, DSOs, MSOs) |

## Step 4: Apply the playbook honestly

The playbooks are dense (60k characters each). Do not dump them. Pull the section that matches the client's stage (pre-launch, early traction, scaling, mature) and apply it.

Each playbook has:
- **Executive thesis**, the north-star metric and the sequence of moves
- **Why this category is different**, the structural mechanics
- **Operating model**, the actual work, in order
- **Metrics and measurement**, the named metrics for this model
- **Failure modes**, what breaks

Ground every recommendation in the loaded playbook. When you cite a framework, metric, or claim, use the source link the playbook provides (a16z, Gartner, NFX, LinkedIn B2B Institute, April Dunford, Hinge, Forrester, Edelman, etc.). Do not fabricate stats or add unsourced numbers.

**When the client's ask conflicts with the playbook, say so and explain why.** Then offer the playbook's approach as the alternative.

## Step 5: Metrics and measurement

Use the playbook's named metrics, not generic funnel metrics:

- **Healthcare / life sciences:** Qualified pipeline, deal velocity by stage, VAC (Value Analysis Committee) stage duration, HIPAA-compliant conversion (server-side) attribution, cost per booked consult
- **B2B SaaS ABM:** Buying-group consensus rate, pipeline coverage, account engagement score, deal velocity, win rate by target-account tier
- **B2B SaaS PLG:** Activation rate, PQL (product-qualified) conversion, NRR, expansion revenue, time-to-value
- **Marketplaces:** Liquidity, match rate, time-to-match, cohort retention, take rate
- **Agencies / professional services:** Referral source diversity, mental availability (Ehrenberg-Bass CEP framework), IP asset production, partner utilization
- **Franchise / multi-location:** Local pipeline per location, HQ-to-local co-op efficiency, brand consistency scores
- **Regulated:** Compliance-approved-asset production velocity, MLR review cycle time, cost per compliant lead

Never substitute vanity metrics. Never report "leads generated" without a downstream conversion cohort attached.

## What NOT to do

- Do not paraphrase a playbook when the user asks what it says, quote or read the relevant section and cite it.
- Do not blend playbooks that don't apply. A B2B SaaS company is not a marketplace even if it has "network effects" in a slide.
- Do not invent metrics, benchmarks, or stats. If the playbook doesn't have the number, search and cite the source.
- Do not skip the compliance/regulatory sections in `playbook-regulated-industries.md` or `playbook-healthcare-life-sciences.md`. Those constraints are not optional.
- Do not recommend engagement structures that violate Step 2 (90-day contracts on long-cycle work, revenue judgment before the sales cycle has finished, no leading/lagging split).
- Do not write client-facing copy without loading `isovertic-voice`.
