# The Franchise and Multi-Location Marketing Playbook

## Executive summary

Franchise and multi-location marketing is not ordinary brand management repeated across more addresses. It is a **distributed demand system**: corporate creates memory, standards, infrastructure and leverage; regional groups translate broad strategy into market-level pressure; local operators convert nearby intent into calls, visits, appointments, memberships and repeat purchases. The winning design is neither total centralization nor “let every owner market.” It is **structured empowerment**—a small, explicit set of non-negotiables surrounded by approved local choices, with common data and consequences.

The commercial stakes are large. IFA projected 851,000 U.S. franchise establishments and more than $936 billion of output in 2025, spanning more than 300 business-format categories ([International Franchise Association](https://www.franchise.org/2025/02/for-second-consecutive-year-franchising-exceeds-economic-expectations-outpaces-broader-economy/)). Yet the operating strain is equally clear: 47% of franchisors in a 2025 study named managing reputation across markets as their largest challenge; 37% cited balancing corporate control with local customization; 34% cited franchisee adherence to brand guidelines ([State of Franchise Marketing](https://assets.ctfassets.net/t21gix3kzulv/4r1afljTtfKU0mWSUmbNoE/7b56a54cc5f91fc6e5a415c3c05e42d3/CT-PDF-State_of_Franchise_Marketing_Report25_-_UPDATE.pdf)).

The recommended system has five layers:

1. **One strategy and one customer truth.** Corporate owns positioning, distinctive assets, audience definitions, core offers, national media, data architecture and measurement rules.
2. **Three execution tiers.** National investment creates mental availability; regional co-ops concentrate spend across sensible media markets; local programs capture demand within actual trade areas.
3. **A governed local operating system.** Every location receives accurate listings, a useful local page, review workflows, approved creative, paid-media guardrails, CRM journeys and a community calendar.
4. **Mutual accountability.** The franchisor publishes fund receipts, uses and results; franchisees meet local standards, fund obligations, response SLAs and data requirements. Unit economics—not impressions—arbitrate disputes. In IFA’s 2025 survey, 42% of executives called unit economics the most important factor in the franchisor-franchisee relationship ([IFA/FRANdata survey](https://www.franchise.org/2025/02/2025-ifa-franchisor-survey-shows-strong-signs-for-growth-amid-continued-economic-constraints/)).
5. **A closed measurement loop.** National MMM, matched-market tests, location-level lead and sales outcomes, same-store cohorts, and an execution scorecard determine what is scaled, fixed or stopped.

The first 90 days should therefore fix ownership and data before launching a major campaign: audit the fund and contracts; inventory every location and profile; establish baselines; interview operators; define decision rights; repair high-intent discovery and review gaps; then test a small number of repeatable local demand plays.

## 1. Why franchise and multi-location marketing is different

### One brand, many local monopolies

A consumer experiences “the brand” through one unit: one clinic, restaurant, studio, dealership, broker, salon or service technician. Corporate reputation can earn consideration, but the location’s hours, map pin, availability, reviews, offer, response speed and customer experience win or lose the transaction. This creates two simultaneous marketing jobs:

- **Build future demand across the system:** fame, salience, distinctive assets, category-entry-point association, sponsorships, national partnerships and broad reach.
- **Convert demand near each unit:** accurate location data, local search visibility, reviews, localized proof, calls, directions, bookings, lead routing, offers and remarketing.

The tasks interact but do not substitute for each other. A high-reach campaign cannot rescue a closed-looking Google profile; perfect listings cannot create a famous brand. Google’s local-search research found that consumers seek practical information such as directions, address and hours, and that 88% of smartphone users in its study conducted local searches ([Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/914/how-advertisers-can-extend-their-relevance-with-search_research-studies.pdf)). Rio SEO’s 2025 study found 84% of consumers search for local businesses online daily and 53% say inaccurate listings can drive them away ([Rio SEO](https://www.rioseo.com/resources/white-paper/2025-local-search-consumer-behavior-study/)).

### The principal–agent tension

Corporate bears systemwide brand risk and sees cross-market data. Franchisees supply capital, know the trade area and feel local cash flow immediately. Corporate therefore tends to favor consistency, pooled scale and longer-term brand effects; an operator may favor this week’s leads, a neighborhood sponsorship or a discount that works locally. Both perspectives contain useful information—and predictable bias.

The governance error is to solve this tension with slogans. “Brand consistency” can hide irrelevant creative, weak reporting or national spend that does not reach smaller markets. “Local autonomy” can hide duplicated costs, poor claims, tiny audiences, fragmented data and off-brand execution. [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2013/03/great-advertising-is-both-loca) warns that advertising must work locally and globally because local mistakes can now damage the whole brand. Deloitte’s analysis of hotel franchising likewise describes a shift toward flexibility rather than treating standardization as the only value proposition ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/Industries/consumer/blogs/from-uniformity-to-flexibility.html)).

### The answer: centralize the irreversible; localize the contextual

Centralize decisions where fragmentation destroys value: trademarks, positioning, identity, claims, martech, customer identity, data definitions, national partnerships, crisis response and experimentation standards. Localize decisions where context dominates: community partners, neighborhood imagery, event timing, local proof, language nuance, service radius and budget allocation among approved plays. Regionalize what depends on media-market scale, seasonality or clusters of contiguous territories.

This is a decision-rights problem, not merely a creative problem. Every recurring activity should have a named owner, approver, service level, funding source, permitted variations and escalation route.

## 2. Step 1 — Build the marketing operating model

### Design the three-tier portfolio

| Tier | Best use | Typical owner | Funding logic | Core outputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| National/corporate | Brand platform, broad reach, sponsorships, martech, research, loyalty, core creative | CMO / brand fund board | Mandatory system fund plus corporate contribution | Brand campaigns, platform, standards, master assets, measurement |
| Regional/co-op | DMA-level reach, seasonality, shared promotions, PR, sports and audio | Regional council with corporate controls | Participating units, often matched or supplemented | Market media plan, regional creative adaptation, market test |
| Local/unit | Capture intent and build neighborhood relevance | Franchisee or local marketer | Required local spend and/or reimbursable co-op | Search, social, listings, reviews, CRM, events, partnerships |

Do not assign channels permanently by tier. Paid search can be national for brand terms, regional for competitive conquesting and local for high-intent service queries. Assign **objectives and audiences**, then prevent overlap through exclusions, shared bidding rules and territory logic.

### Fund economics and transparency

Many systems assess roughly 2–4% of gross sales for advertising, although actual FDD terms vary materially ([Goodway Group](https://goodwaygroup.com/blog/franchise-co-op-advertising)). Treat that range as an external reference, not a prescription. Required spend must be derived from category economics, brand maturity, geography, competitive intensity and the work the fund is expected to perform. A system asking one fund to finance broadcast, production, loyalty technology, local tools, agency fees and franchise sales will almost certainly underfund each. National-fund guidance from the franchise sector likewise recommends tying a limited set of deliverables to explicit priorities and showing operators the value created ([Franchising.com](https://www.franchising.com/articles/what_every_franchisor_should_know_about_national_marketing_funds.html)).

A practical fund design separates:

- **Working media** by national, regional and local purpose.
- **Non-working marketing cost**: production, agency, data, research, platforms and measurement.
- **Lifecycle reserves** for openings, crises, seasonality and experiments.
- **Corporate-funded obligations** that should not be quietly transferred to franchisees, such as investor communications or general overhead.

BDO recommends separate accounts or ledger treatment and clear documentation of receipts and disbursements to preserve the fund’s paper trail ([BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/industries/restaurants/best-practices-for-franchisor-cooperative-advertising-funds)). Publish a quarterly one-page fund statement: opening balance, contributions by source, expenses by objective/channel/vendor, committed but unspent funds, outcomes, tests, and next-quarter allocation. Also publish the allocation policy: whether company-owned units contribute on equivalent terms, whether the franchisor may use funds for administration or franchise recruitment, whether unused money rolls over, and how rebates are treated. FTC buyer guidance explicitly tells prospects to examine what percentage of the fund goes to national versus local advertising, administrative cost, and selling more franchises ([Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumers-guide-buying-franchise)).

Sterling-led franchise research also finds a recurring gap between headquarters strategy and the local execution burden, reinforcing that transparency must extend to workflows, not only money ([Uberall and Greg Sterling](https://uberall.com/en-us/resources/blog/the-key-tenets-of-digital-marketing-for-franchises)). The risk is not theoretical. Franchise Times’ history of Subway recounts litigation over control of the franchisee advertising fund, illustrating how unclear control can become a system-level conflict ([Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/article_archive/subway-s-turning-point/article_d64b07c8-cdef-555b-9b70-972f59a9d945.html)). Papa Johns provides a more recent strategic lesson: after moving contributions from local marketing toward a larger national fund, it restored local allocations amid sales weakness ([Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/franchise_news/papa-johns-returns-to-local-marketing-amid-sales-slump/article_bf3483e2-d525-11ef-aafa-17d554b94d24.html)). The lesson is not that local always beats national; it is that the portfolio needs enough funding for both memory creation and market conversion.

### Decision rights and enforcement

Build a RACI-style charter with four classes:

- **Mandatory:** trademark use, legal claims, promotion terms, data capture, platform access, profile accuracy, brand safety and crisis communications.
- **Pre-approved:** templates, offers, channels, vendors, audiences, sponsorship categories and local PR formats that can run without case-by-case review.
- **Approval required:** new claims, material discounts, custom creative, influencers, political/cause partnerships, new vendors or out-of-territory media.
- **Prohibited:** unsubstantiated earnings/health claims, unlicensed assets, fake reviews, audience discrimination, shadow domains, unapproved logos and unmanaged customer lists.

The IFA’s legal presentation emphasizes truthful, non-deceptive and substantiated advertising and explains that FDD disclosures cover marketing funds and local obligations ([International Franchise Association](https://www.franchise.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/IFA-Legal-2024-Marketing-Funds-and-Local-Marketing_2.pdf)). Enforcement should be graduated: automated warning, correction window, coaching, loss of reimbursement, then contractual remedy for repeated or material violations. A help desk and fast approvals must accompany enforcement; otherwise governance becomes a bottleneck.

### Approved vendors without a captive-market trap

Maintain two lanes: certified full-service partners and self-service tools. Certification should test data security, API access, territory controls, brand templates, reporting portability, accessibility, service levels and exit provisions. Rebid material categories periodically; disclose referral economics; preserve franchisor access to raw account and campaign data. IFA maintains supplier and preferred-vendor resources, but brand-level approval still requires due diligence against the system’s own requirements ([IFA Preferred Vendors](https://www.franchise.org/ifa-preferred-vendors/)).

## 3. Step 2 — Create a brand system built for scale

### Write a usable brand book

A franchise brand book must be an operating interface, not a 100-page identity memorial. Include:

- Positioning, audience, promise, reasons to believe and category-entry points.
- Distinctive assets: name, logo, color, shape, sonic cues, characters, phrases, product/service rituals and spatial cues.
- Voice, message hierarchy, claims library and proof requirements.
- Photography rules, inclusion/accessibility standards and local-image guidance.
- Channel examples for listings, paid social, search, email/SMS, signage, direct mail, sponsorship and PR.
- “Fixed / flexible / forbidden” annotations on every template.
- Version control, expiration dates, rights/usage terms and an exception process.

Ehrenberg-Bass defines useful distinctive assets through **uniqueness** and **fame** and advises managers to measure asset strength rather than assume a color, logo or font is recognized ([Ehrenberg-Bass Institute](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/brands-of-distinction)). Conduct periodic prompted and unprompted asset research. Protect strong assets consistently; invest to build promising ones; stop decorating campaigns with weak, generic cues.

### Build modular creative, not rigid sameness

The scalable unit is a component system: locked brand frame, approved headlines and calls to action, local proof modules, offer fields, location details, image choices and mandatory disclosures. Each asset should be automatically assembled into channel-safe sizes with localization rules. A dental group might lock trust and clinical standards while allowing local doctor photography; an HVAC system might lock guarantee language while allowing climate- and service-area modules; a QSR might lock food photography while allowing regional items and local-event panels.

QSR Magazine’s multi-brand local marketing guidance calls for a framework standardized enough to scale yet flexible enough to reflect brand priorities, reinforced internally before franchisees are asked to adopt it ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/how-a-multi-brand-restaurant-company-can-win-with-local-store-marketing/)). The right KPI is not “assets downloaded.” It is percentage of locations running compliant, current creative and the incremental outcome those deployments produce.

### DAM and local marketing portal requirements

The digital asset management layer should provide role-based permissions, metadata by market/channel/language, rights expiration, automatic archiving, template editing, approval workflow, co-op claim documentation and usage analytics. Integrate it with the paid-media platform and store data so an address, phone number or offer is not retyped. Retire old assets automatically. Search should work in franchisee language—“spring tune-up ad,” not internal campaign codes.

Use adoption telemetry as product research: zero-use templates may be hard to find, locally irrelevant or operationally unrealistic. Hold quarterly “franchisee build sessions” in which operators complete a real campaign while the central team observes friction.

## 4. Step 3 — Win local SEO at scale

### Establish a location source of truth

Create one canonical location record containing name, address, phone, URL, categories, coordinates, hours, holiday hours, opening date, amenities, service area, booking/order links, products/services, accessibility data and temporary-closure status. Feed it to Google Business Profile (GBP), Apple, Bing, major directories, navigation platforms, store locator, paid media and analytics. The governing principle is not merely NAP consistency; it is **entity consistency** across all customer and machine-readable fields.

Google permits eligible chains to pursue bulk verification with 10 or more profiles and recommends a business group account for shared management ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4490296?hl=en)). Keep corporate as primary owner, give role-appropriate access, prohibit personal-email ownership, log changes and define recovery procedures. Service-area businesses need a separate workflow because Google’s bulk process excludes them.

### Choose the platform around workflow, not logo count

Yext, Uberall, Rio SEO, Birdeye and SOCi overlap but have different strengths across listings distribution, duplicate suppression, reviews, social, local pages, analytics and managed services. Run a scored pilot using real failure cases:

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| Data propagation | Change holiday hours; measure accuracy and latency across priority endpoints |
| Governance | Can corporate lock fields while operators propose local edits? |
| Exceptions | Can it handle service areas, departments, practitioners, relocations and temporary closures? |
| Reviews | Does it route, alert, draft, approve and report by location and topic? |
| Local pages | Can it render fast, indexable, schema-marked pages without thin duplication? |
| Reporting | Does it expose raw data/API and location cohorts, not only a proprietary score? |
| AI discovery | Can it monitor citations/mentions in emerging answer engines without pretending the metric is settled? |

Yext’s listings proposition is centralized control and direct distribution of verified facts ([Yext](https://www.yext.com/platform/listings)); Uberall reports that regularly updated GBP profiles can receive five times more views in its cited research ([Uberall](https://uberall.com/en-us/resources/blog/must-know-local-seo-statistics-for-your-business-growth)). Vendor claims should be validated in a 20–50-location pilot against manual audits and business outcomes.

### Build location pages that deserve to rank

Every location gets one canonical, server-rendered URL linked from a crawlable store locator. Include unique title/meta, address, embedded map, local phone, current hours, booking/order CTA, services/products, service radius where relevant, staff/provider details, accepted insurance or payment information, parking/accessibility, local testimonials, FAQs, photographs and LocalBusiness schema. Cross-link nearby locations without creating doorway pages.

Thin city-swap pages are a liability. Whitespark’s local ranking guidance recommends pages that prove local expertise with genuinely relevant entities and content, while warning that inaccurate public hours frustrate customers and violate guidance ([Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)). Google’s Locator Plus documentation provides a reference implementation for searchable maps, place details and directions ([Google Maps Platform](https://developers.google.com/maps/solutions/store-locator)).

For each page, track organic impressions by non-brand versus brand query, local-pack rank grid, calls, directions, bookings/orders, form completion, assisted conversions and page freshness. Do not rank franchisees by raw traffic without adjusting for trade-area demand, maturity and competition.

### Operate a daily/weekly/monthly hygiene cadence

- **Daily:** ingest suggested edits, closures, suspensions and critical inaccuracies; monitor duplicate or hijacked profiles.
- **Weekly:** sync changed hours/offers; answer Q&A; publish meaningful updates; review rank and conversion anomalies.
- **Monthly:** audit categories, URLs, tracking, photos, attributes, duplicates, local-page integrity and competitor visibility.
- **Quarterly:** test category strategy, local content and conversion flows; compare provider data with spot checks.

SOCi’s 2024 Local Visibility Index found high-visibility brands appeared on page one 53.2% of the time versus 23.6% for average brands; top-three positions produced 126% more traffic and 93% more actions than ranks four through ten ([SOCi Local Visibility Index](https://www.soci.ai/blog/top-takeaways-from-the-2024-soci-local-visibility-index-lvi/)). Treat rankings as leading indicators and actions/sales as outcomes.

## 5. Step 4 — Manage reputation as a distributed response system

### Reviews are demand evidence and operating telemetry

Only 4% of consumers in BrightLocal’s 2025 survey said they never read online business reviews ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)). Rio SEO found 75% read at least four reviews and 59% expect a business response within 24 hours ([Rio SEO](https://www.rioseo.com/resources/white-paper/2025-local-search-consumer-behavior-study/)). ReviewTrackers reports that 94% have avoided a business because of a bad review ([ReviewTrackers](https://www.reviewtrackers.com/reports/customer-reviews-stats/)). These are vendor studies with differing samples and methods, so avoid false precision; the consistent strategic conclusion is that rating, volume, recency, content and response behavior all matter.

Womply’s observational analysis found above-average review counts correlated with 82% higher annual revenue than below-average counts and that businesses responding to reviews averaged 35% more revenue ([Search Engine Land summary of Womply research](https://searchengineland.com/review-counts-matter-more-to-local-business-revenue-than-star-ratings-according-to-study-320271)). Correlation is not causation—well-run businesses may both earn reviews and earn more—but the low cost and diagnostic value justify a systematic program.

### Define the review operating model

1. **Request:** trigger a neutral request after a genuine interaction, preferably through POS/CRM/booking events. Never gate by satisfaction or reward only positive reviews.
2. **Route:** consolidate Google and category platforms by location; tag topics such as wait time, staff, quality, cleanliness, billing and availability.
3. **Respond:** target 24 hours for negative or safety-sensitive reviews and 48 hours for routine positive reviews; regulated categories require approved language and privacy controls.
4. **Recover:** move case-specific resolution off-platform, create a ticket, assign an owner and close the loop.
5. **Learn:** publish recurring topic trends to marketing and operating leaders without exposing personal information.

Google requires a verified profile before a business can reply and makes responses public under the business identity ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)). Therefore corporate should own access and policy; trained local teams should supply context; central or managed-service responders should handle volume. Use AI to classify and draft, never to fabricate specifics or auto-publish sensitive clinical, legal, safety or discrimination responses.

### Score both customer outcome and process

A location scorecard should show rating, review count per 100 transactions/customers, 30/90-day recency, response rate, median response time, unresolved critical reviews, topic mix and rating trend. Benchmark within comparable cohorts, not the whole system. SOCi’s index found leading brands responded to 80.5% of Google reviews versus 45.1% for average brands ([SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/blog/top-takeaways-from-the-2024-soci-local-visibility-index-lvi/)). Set a realistic improvement path, then raise the standard.

## 6. Step 5 — Scale local paid media without losing control

### Build one account architecture

Corporate should control business manager/admin access, conversion definitions, pixels/tags, offline-conversion imports, audiences, naming, exclusions and billing policy. Campaigns should identify brand, market, location, objective, offer and creative version. Prevent units from bidding against each other or corporate by assigning territory polygons/radii, negative locations, brand-keyword rules and suppression logic.

The program needs three modes:

- **Always-on capture:** search, maps/store goals, Local Services Ads where relevant, retargeting and branded defense.
- **Demand creation:** local video/social/display using approved audience and creative modules.
- **Event/promotion bursts:** openings, seasonal demand, classes, consultations, limited-time offers and community events.

Google’s Performance Max for store goals can optimize toward store visits, directions and local actions using linked location assets ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12971048?hl=en)). Use business outcomes where available; local actions are proxies, not revenue.

### Offer guided self-service, not blank media accounts

Platforms such as Tiger Pistol and Evocalize package central controls with local activation. Kenshoo is now Skai, so evaluate the current Skai product against the same distributed-local requirements rather than relying on the legacy “Kenshoo Local” label ([Skai](https://skai.io/blog/welcome-to-skai/)). Tiger Pistol describes 100% brand control across Wendy’s U.S. franchisees in its case material ([Tiger Pistol](https://tigerpistol.com/franchise-brands/)); Evocalize reports portfolio examples including lower customer-acquisition cost, but these are vendor case claims and should be independently tested ([Evocalize](https://evocalize.com/franchise/)). Birdeye’s multi-location guidance similarly frames central monitoring, location comparison and scalable response as core reputation capabilities ([Birdeye](https://birdeye.com/blog/multi-location-reputation-management/)). The platform decision should hinge on whether a franchisee can launch a correct campaign in minutes while corporate retains data, budget limits and kill switches.

Create “plays,” not channel menus: “fill Tuesday appointments,” “spring HVAC tune-up,” “new member trial,” “lapsed guest reactivation.” Each play specifies eligible locations, objective, minimum audience, offer, creative, budget range, duration, landing destination and KPI. Local operators choose among relevant plays and may add approved local context.

### Co-op mechanics that get used

Reimbursement-heavy programs fail when operators must become media buyers and accountants. Prefer direct-funded or pre-approved platform spend. If claims are necessary, show balance, eligible tactics and deadlines in one portal; require proof automatically; pay quickly. Many co-op arrangements require pre-approval and documented spend ([Goodway Group](https://goodwaygroup.com/blog/franchise-co-op-advertising)). Track utilization, but diagnose non-use: low adoption may signal insufficient cash flow, poor relevance, bad UX or lack of confidence—not apathy.

Use a minimum viable budget based on expected conversion volume. If a unit cannot generate enough signal, pool at the regional level rather than fragmenting spend into dozens of underpowered campaigns.

## 7. Step 6 — Use national campaigns when scale creates an advantage

National brand investment becomes rational when enough distribution exists to monetize broad reach, brand assets are consistent, the offer is operationally available, and locations can capture demand. Spend should build broad mental availability across likely future buyers, not merely retarget known customers. Sponsorship, broadcast, streaming video, audio, creators, PR and large cultural moments can generate shared memory and reduce the cost of local conversion.

Binet and Field’s often-cited 60:40 brand-building-to-activation ratio is a starting hypothesis, not franchise law; their work explicitly adjusts the balance for context ([Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/binet-field-reveal-key-formulas-brand-building/1495641)). Younger, lower-awareness or infrequently purchased brands may need more brand building; urgent, local and high-intent categories may lean toward activation while still maintaining memory structures. Apply the principle—protect long and short effects—rather than worship the ratio.

A Super Bowl spot is justified only when creative fame, PR, digital amplification, franchisee activation and longitudinal measurement form one system. Restaurant Dive’s analysis found modest immediate foot-traffic results for some QSR advertisers, such as 3.3% for Wendy’s and 0.5% for Jack in the Box, and cautioned against assuming expensive reach creates immediate visits ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/super-bowl-qsr-ads-dont-always-lead-to-meaningful-traffic-gains/547455/)). Evaluate awareness, search lift, earned media, new-customer penetration and longer-horizon sales—not opening-week traffic alone.

Before every national launch, pass a “last mile” gate: correct local profiles and pages, in-stock offer, trained responders, booking/order capacity, approved local extensions, CRM capture and crisis plan. A national campaign that overwhelms units or sends demand to broken endpoints destroys fund credibility.

## 8. Step 7 — Make CRM, loyalty and first-party data a system asset

### Create a shared customer contract

The brand should define the customer identity, consent language, preference center, data taxonomy, security, retention, access and permitted local use. Franchisees need timely unit-level insights and activation tools; corporate needs network-level identity resolution, suppression, frequency control and measurement. The contract should prevent local exports to unmanaged tools while making the governed platform more useful than workarounds.

Integrate POS, ecommerce/order, booking, call tracking, lead management, website/app, loyalty, email/SMS and paid-media conversions. The minimum record links customer, location, transaction/lead, channel, consent and timestamp. Preserve both **home location** and **behavioral location** so travelers or movers are not trapped in one unit’s database.

### Loyalty must change behavior profitably

Design around frequency, cross-category adoption, retention and direct ordering—not indiscriminate discounting. Use earn/burn economics, incremental-margin tests, breakage assumptions and franchisee settlement rules. Clarify who funds rewards redeemed at a different operator’s unit. Provide local activation slots within national journeys while controlling frequency and offer conflict.

Domino’s reported that more than 80% of U.S. retail sales in 2022 came through digital channels and 99% of its stores were independently franchised at Q2 2023, demonstrating that franchise ownership need not prevent systemwide digital identity and loyalty ([Domino’s investor relations](https://ir.dominos.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dominosr-loyalty-program-just-became-more-rewarding)). Starbucks’ Q1 FY2025 release shows why loyalty dashboards must sit beside comparable sales and transactions, not replace them: global comparable sales and transactions declined even amid a large digital ecosystem ([Starbucks investor relations](https://investor.starbucks.com/news/financial-releases/news-details/2025/Starbucks-Reports-Q1-Fiscal-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx)). Panera reported nearly 48 million MyPanera members in 2022 and described rewards based on frequency, spend and preferences, demonstrating the scale of first-party identity but not, by itself, incremental value ([Panera Bread](https://www.panerabread.com/en-us/press/press-room/panera-expands-mypanera-loyalty-program.html)). Panera-style subscription or frequency programs should therefore be judged on incremental visits, contribution margin and retention cohorts, not enrollment headlines.

### Essential lifecycle journeys

Run centrally governed, locally attributable journeys for welcome, second purchase, lapsed customer, replenishment/service interval, appointment reminder, abandoned booking/order, birthday/anniversary, review request and win-back. Hold out a control group so “revenue from messages” is not confused with purchases that would have happened anyway. Return unit-level lists and insights only within permissions and privacy rules.

## 9. Step 8 — Enable franchisees as a marketing channel

A playbook unused is not a capability. Build enablement around franchisee jobs-to-be-done and operator time constraints. QSR Magazine recommends easy-to-digest communication and tools that let operators focus on human connections rather than production complexity ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/how-a-multi-brand-restaurant-company-can-win-with-local-store-marketing/)).

The enablement stack should contain:

- Role-based onboarding for owner, general manager and local marketer.
- Short LMS modules with certification on brand, reviews, social, leads, data/privacy and co-op rules.
- A searchable playbook indexed by goal and category.
- Monthly campaign kits delivered early enough to plan staffing and inventory.
- Office hours, ticketed help desk and escalation paths with published SLAs.
- Done-for-you services for small or under-resourced units.
- Peer councils and a tested-practices library showing context, cost and result.
- Field-marketing coaches who diagnose performance rather than merely inspect compliance.

Adoption should be observable: login, certification, campaign launch, lead response, review response, local-event completion and data-quality scores. Segment support. A high-performing multi-unit operator may need strategic experimentation; a new single-unit owner needs a calendar and execution help; a persistently noncompliant unit needs remediation.

Make franchisee feedback consequential. Maintain a council with rotating representation by geography, tenure, scale and performance. Preview campaigns, show what input changed and explain what did not. McKinsey’s interview with Flynn Group emphasizes evolutionary change, sharing practices across teams and combining consumer signals with operational response rather than treating campaigns alone as sufficient ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/retail/our-insights/how-the-worlds-largest-restaurant-franchise-operator-uses-ai)).

## 10. Step 9 — Run every new-unit opening as a measured launch

An opening is a rare moment when local PR, paid reach, community curiosity and CRM acquisition reinforce each other. Use a stage-gated kit.

### 8–12 weeks before opening

Confirm canonical location data, page, map pin, tracking numbers, conversion events, GBP eligibility, social permissions, photography, trade area, customer personas, community calendar and lead-capture destination. Recruit a founding list through “coming soon” ads, construction signage, local partners, employer outreach and waitlist/referral offers. Do not publish hours or an opening date until operations can honor them.

### 4–6 weeks before opening

Begin local PR and creator outreach; seed partnerships with schools, employers, chambers, clubs, property managers or healthcare referral sources as category-appropriate. Launch reach/video and high-intent search. Train staff on offer terms, lead response, review requests, customer consent and event roles. Test calls, forms, bookings, orders, CRM routing and offline-conversion uploads.

### Opening week

Use preview/VIP events to find problems before peak volume. Stage content capture, community partner moments and local media. Sequence offers to protect capacity; avoid a giant giveaway that creates one-day bargain traffic and poor reviews. QSR Magazine’s opening guidance stresses matching the event to concept and audience and using trackable social targeting based on known customer profiles ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/how-ace-grand-opening/)).

### Weeks 2–12: ramp, do not disappear

Shift from reach to conversion and retention. Trigger second-visit journeys, review requests and referral offers. Compare actual customers with the pre-opening model. Track lead-to-appointment, appointment-to-sale, first-to-second purchase, cohort retention, rating/volume, local visibility, CAC and weekly sales against a maturity curve. Maintain a matched set of established and recently opened units so “good launch” is not defined by raw revenue alone.

The opening kit should define responsible owners, budgets, deadlines and fallback plans. A postmortem at day 30 and day 90 updates the next opening’s playbook.

## 11. Step 10 — Separate franchise development marketing from consumer marketing

Franchise development is a high-consideration B2B acquisition funnel with different audiences, claims, economics and compliance. Its job is to recruit qualified operators who fit the model—not maximize form fills.

### Build the funnel around qualification

1. **Market and persona:** ideal operator, investment range, experience, geography, ownership model and disqualifiers.
2. **Demand creation:** founder story, category insight, operator proof, earned media, broker education and targeted B2B media.
3. **Capture:** transparent investment range and process, territory context, educational content and a short first form.
4. **Nurture:** brand story, operating model, support, owner interviews, webinars, validation preparation and next steps.
5. **Qualification:** liquidity, experience, cultural fit, territory, timeline and decision group.
6. **Disclosure and diligence:** controlled handoff, compliant FDD delivery and tracked milestones.
7. **Close and learn:** cost per qualified candidate, progression, time to close, source quality and first-year cohort quality.

IFA’s 2025 survey found 54% of responding franchisors planned to use internal franchise sales teams for growth ([IFA](https://www.franchise.org/2025/02/2025-ifa-franchisor-survey-shows-strong-signs-for-growth-amid-continued-economic-constraints/)). Whether leads come from an internal team, brokers, events, Entrepreneur, Franchise.com or paid media, source governance should be consistent. The Franchise Brokers Association is one intermediary ecosystem ([FBA](https://www.franchiseba.com/)); Franchise.com provides discovery and matching ([Franchise.com](https://www.franchise.com/)); Entrepreneur’s Franchise 500 requires documentation and verification but explicitly says the ranking is not an endorsement ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/franchises/entrepreneurs-franchise-500-how-we-decide-who-makes-the/253977)). Treat portal volume and ranking badges as channels, not validation of candidate quality.

### Keep claims inside the compliance lane

Marketing must not imply earnings, payback, margins or success outside permitted, supportable disclosures. The FTC Franchise Rule requires prospective franchisees to receive disclosure documents at least 14 days before signing or paying, while state rules may add requirements ([Federal Trade Commission Franchise Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/federal_register_notices/franchise-rule-16-cfr-part-436/991022franchis.pdf)). Establish legal-approved claims, mandatory footnotes, asset expiration and a log of every ad/landing-page version. Train brokers and agencies; mystery-shop lead handling; monitor affiliates using brand terms.

Measure cost per **qualified opportunity**, discovery-day attendance, FDD receipt, close, opening and first-year survival/performance—not cost per lead. Feed reasons for disqualification back into targeting and creative.

## 12. Step 11 — Turn community marketing into a repeatable local advantage

Community marketing works because franchise locations are physically and socially embedded. It should produce relationships, relevant reach, content and measurable customer action—not a random donation budget.

Create approved playbooks for schools and teams, employer partnerships, chambers, first responders, nonprofits, apartment/property communities, referral professionals and local events. Modern Restaurant Management recommends networking with local businesses and using community activity to make a franchise feel hometown-local while retaining recognizable brand benefits ([Modern Restaurant Management](https://modernrestaurantmanagement.com/10-tips-to-make-your-franchise-feel-local/)). Its local-store-marketing guidance also stresses persistent, year-round relationship building rather than isolated events ([Modern Restaurant Management](https://modernrestaurantmanagement.com/local-store-marketing-lsm-is-it-too-hard-or-are-restaurants-too-lazy/)).

Evaluate opportunities with a simple score: audience fit, geographic overlap, exclusivity, activation rights, content rights, lead capture, staff effort, brand/safety risk and measurable next action. Prefer partnerships that can recur. A gym may create a school-faculty wellness series; an urgent-care group may support employer flu education; a roofer may build realtor/property-manager referral education; a QSR may host repeat school fundraisers.

Give operators assets: outreach email, one-page proposal, event checklist, QR/UTM generator, consent language, photo release, PR template and follow-up journey. Require a post-event record—cost, attendance, leads, redemptions, content, partner feedback and recommended repeat/stop decision. Community activity should enrich local pages and social proof without manufacturing “local” content centrally.

## 13. Step 12 — Measure the system, not isolated channels

### Use a KPI tree

**Enterprise outcome:** incremental system sales or contribution margin, customer growth and brand health.

**National leading indicators:** aided/unaided awareness, distinctive-asset recognition, category-entry-point association, share of search, reach/frequency and new-customer penetration.

**Local demand indicators:** local-pack visibility, share of local voice, profile actions, local-page conversion, qualified leads, calls, bookings, directions, CAC and cost per incremental sale.

**Retention:** identifiable-customer rate, enrollment, active members, purchase frequency, churn, cross-location use, incremental loyalty margin and CRM holdout lift.

**Execution:** profile completeness/freshness, review response SLA, campaign adoption, lead response, template compliance, training completion and data integrity.

**Unit economics:** same-store sales, transactions/leads, ticket, gross/contribution margin and payback. Report cohorts by tenure, format, ownership and market potential.

### Combine three methods

1. **Marketing mix modeling:** Use national or regional time-series variation to estimate broad-channel contribution, control for price, distribution, promotions, seasonality, economy and competition, and examine lag/saturation.
2. **Geo experiments:** Hold out matched markets or locations, pre-register the design, prevent contamination and estimate incrementality. Wayfair’s description shows why optimized smaller holdouts may be preferable to withholding half the country ([Wayfair](https://www.aboutwayfair.com/careers/tech-blog/how-wayfair-uses-geo-experiments-to-measure-incrementality)).
3. **User-level experiments:** Use CRM holdouts, conversion lift and offer tests where identity and consent permit. Never treat last-click attribution as incrementality.

Google research on geo-level Bayesian hierarchical MMM recommends normalizing by geo population and partially pooling information across geographies, which is useful when individual locations have sparse data ([Google Research](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/3804.pdf)). In practice, cluster low-volume units and borrow strength across comparable markets while preserving local variation.

### Create a franchisee scorecard that invites action

The monthly page should answer: What happened? Why? What action comes next? Show system benchmark, peer cohort, target and trend for a limited set of controllable metrics. Link each red metric to a play. Never use proprietary vanity scores as the only evidence. For local search, Share of Local Voice expresses how often a location appears across a geographic rank grid; Local Falcon’s definition makes it useful for seeing neighborhood blind spots ([Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/knowledge-base/kb32-what-is-solv-how-to-interpret-and-use-the-metric)). Pair it with calls, bookings and sales.

Protect analytical honesty: predefine success, show confidence intervals where feasible, disclose missing data, separate correlation from causation, and report negative tests. The fund earns trust by stopping low-value work as visibly as it celebrates wins.

## 14. Calibrate the system by business model

The operating system stays constant, but the local conversion event, radius, proof and cadence vary by category. Do not force restaurants, urgent care and roofing into one “local marketing” benchmark.

### QSR and restaurant chains

The scarce resources are nearby attention, occasion relevance and operating capacity. Corporate should own food/product imagery, menu claims, loyalty, national promotions and delivery/order integrations; local teams should emphasize dayparts, catering, neighborhood partnerships, events and genuinely local social content. Optimize local pages and profiles for menu, hours, order, directions, amenities and holiday changes. Use transaction and item-level POS data to measure incrementality, separating ticket from traffic. Nation’s Restaurant News has documented Wendy’s expansion of localized TikTok execution, an example of a national platform enabling market-level relevance rather than choosing one or the other ([Nation’s Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/wendy-s-expands-localized-tiktok-marketing-platform)). QSR franchisees interviewed by QSR Magazine reduce the idea to consistent presence and deep community connection, while still balancing the brand system ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/lessons-in-local-store-marketing-from-two-restaurant-franchisees/)).

### Fitness, gyms and studios

Optimize for trials, booked intros, show rate, membership conversion, 30/90-day retention and class utilization—not cheap leads. Local creative can feature coaches, members with releases, challenges and schedule availability; corporate should govern body-image claims, trademarks, offer terms and privacy. Radius and message should differ by commuter pattern and format. CRM speed matters: route leads to the studio, automate immediate acknowledgment, and measure human follow-up. Community partnerships with employers, schools, races and health professionals should feed trackable trial journeys. Paid media should suppress active members and distinguish brand search from net-new category demand.

### Home services

Roofing, HVAC, cleaning, plumbing and restoration are service-area businesses, often with seasonal or emergency demand. Territory accuracy, call handling, Local Services Ads, licenses, guarantees, financing disclosures, technician proof and weather/event triggers matter more than foot traffic. Google’s bulk-verification process does not cover service-area businesses, so governance must explicitly handle profile eligibility and address-hiding rules ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4490296?hl=en)). Build useful service-area pages rather than cloned city pages; Whitespark recommends locally relevant evidence and expertise instead of thin “near me” content ([Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)). Measure qualified calls, booked jobs, revenue, gross margin, cancellation and service radius—not forms alone.

### Automotive

Separate sales, service, parts and collision journeys while presenting one coherent location entity. Inventory, model/service availability, appointment links, financing or price claims, recall/service reminders and review topics require structured feeds. Regional media can create reach around dealer clusters; local search and CRM convert shoppers and owners. Measure appointment set, show, repair order, lead-to-sale and repeat service. Do not let each dealer create competing domains or inconsistent offers that confuse users and search engines.

### Hair, beauty and personal care

Discovery is visual and practitioner-specific. Corporate should protect visual identity, claims, offer economics and booking data; salons can localize portfolios, staff bios, availability, trend content and community partnerships. Practitioner pages should be governed so departures do not orphan customer data or produce stale content. Reputation monitoring must separate service quality, wait, cleanliness and individual-provider themes. Rio SEO found consumers inspect multiple information surfaces and care about accurate local facts, reinforcing the need for synchronized booking, hours and profiles ([Rio SEO](https://www.rioseo.com/news/83-of-local-consumers-conduct-online-searches-daily-according-to-new-rio-seo-research/)).

### Tax preparation and real estate brokerages

Both combine local trust with episodic, high-consideration demand. Tax marketing requires intense seasonal pacing, appointment capacity, credential proof and carefully substantiated refund or savings claims. Real estate requires agent and office governance, neighborhood expertise, listing/feed integrity and fair-housing-compliant audiences and creative. Corporate should own compliance, national trust and shared CRM; offices and agents provide local market evidence. Hyperlocal content must help a buyer or taxpayer, not exist solely to rank.

### Urgent care, dental and other healthcare networks

Accuracy, privacy and clinical claims are non-negotiable. Profiles and pages must clarify services, age limits, insurance, accessibility, current hours, appointment/walk-in process and emergency boundaries. Review responses must never reveal protected information; move resolution offline. Paid media and CRM should use approved health-category targeting and consent rules. Local reputation themes can guide messaging and experience improvements, but central teams should review sensitive responses. The IFA legal guidance that claims must be truthful and substantiated applies with particular force in regulated categories ([International Franchise Association](https://www.franchise.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/IFA-Legal-2024-Marketing-Funds-and-Local-Marketing_2.pdf)).

### Portfolio implications

Franchise Brands’ marketing ecosystem perspective argues for synchronizing brand, consumer and development functions rather than allowing channel and team silos to fragment growth ([Franchising.com](https://www.franchising.com/articles/20260526_from_silos_to_sync_unleashing_franchise_growth_across_the_marketing_eco.html)). Create market archetypes rather than custom strategy for every unit: urban walk-in, suburban destination, rural service area, commuter, seasonal/tourist, mature/high-awareness and new/low-awareness. Assign default media mix, radius, creative modules, CRM cadence and benchmark cohort to each archetype, then allow documented exceptions. The State of Franchise Marketing report’s finding that 47% struggle with reputation across markets and 37% with control versus customization supports this middle path ([State of Franchise Marketing](https://assets.ctfassets.net/t21gix3kzulv/4r1afljTtfKU0mWSUmbNoE/7b56a54cc5f91fc6e5a415c3c05e42d3/CT-PDF-State_of_Franchise_Marketing_Report25_-_UPDATE.pdf)).

AI changes interfaces, not the governance requirement. Greg Sterling’s Uberall research describes the persistent split in which headquarters dictates strategy while franchisees carry local execution, with listings and reviews major challenges for both groups ([Uberall franchise research](https://uberall.com/en-us/resources/blog/the-key-tenets-of-digital-marketing-for-franchises)). Monitor AI answers and citations, but first make location facts, pages and reputation reliable. SOCi’s benchmark found only 1.2% of locations recommended by ChatGPT and 11% by Gemini in its sample, while 35.9% appeared in Google’s local 3-pack—evidence that AI visibility is harder and still developing ([SOCi benchmarks](https://www.soci.ai/blog/local-visibility-benchmarks/)).

## 15. Common anti-patterns and corrections

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Starving the ad fund | Production, technology and media compete for an inadequate pool; every layer underperforms | Cost the required jobs separately; set a floor for experimentation and measurement; publish trade-offs |
| Treating the fund as opaque corporate money | Franchisees cannot connect contribution to outcomes, encouraging conflict and noncompliance | Quarterly receipts/use/outcome reporting, advisory council, disclosed allocation and audit rights |
| Swinging between all-national and all-local | National reach misses conversion; fragmented local spend never builds memory | Explicit portfolio by objective, market scale and maturity |
| Letting each unit build its own brand | Inconsistent cues weaken recognition; claims and rights create legal risk | Distinctive-asset rules, modular templates, fast approvals and graduated enforcement |
| “One-size-fits-all” creative | It ignores category, climate, culture, supply, maturity and customer proof | Fixed brand frame plus approved local modules; test variants by market archetype |
| Centralizing every local decision | Corporate becomes a queue and misses context | Pre-approved plays and decision thresholds; local choice within guardrails |
| Buying local-tech shelfware | Franchisees face too many logins and unclear workflows | Integrate around jobs-to-be-done; pilot usability; measure activation and outcomes |
| Ignoring listings hygiene | Paid and brand demand lands on wrong hours, phone numbers or URLs | Canonical location data, bulk ownership, change SLAs and automated audits |
| Creating thin location pages | Swapped city names add no customer value and compete with one another | Unique proof, services, staff, FAQs, schema, conversion and local content |
| Chasing star rating alone | It invites gating and neglects volume, recency, themes and recovery | Neutral requests, review-rate denominator, SLA, topic analysis and case closure |
| Automating review replies blindly | Hallucinated or insensitive responses create privacy and reputation risk | AI drafts with rules, human review for sensitive topics and escalation taxonomy |
| Hyperlocal targeting without volume | Tiny campaigns cannot learn; CPM/CAC rises | Pool contiguous units or use regional campaigns with local creative/landing pages |
| Franchisee reimbursement bureaucracy | Eligible funds go unused and reporting arrives late | Direct platform funding, visible balances, auto-proof and short payment SLAs |
| National campaign before last-mile readiness | Demand encounters broken profiles, stockouts or untrained staff | Launch readiness gate across listings, pages, capacity, CRM and training |
| Loyalty as permanent discounting | It buys subsidized behavior and creates cross-unit settlement disputes | Incremental-margin tests, earn/burn governance and funded redemption rules |
| Measuring marketing with last click | It overcredits capture channels and undercredits brand/media | MMM + geo tests + user holdouts; business outcomes and lag windows |
| Ranking units by raw outcomes | Market size, tenure and competition swamp execution quality | Peer cohorts, potential adjustment and separate outcome/execution scores |
| Mixing franchise sales with consumer marketing | Audience, claims and conversion economics differ; compliance risk rises | Separate funnel, team, CRM stages, claims library and source-quality KPIs |
| Launch-and-leave openings | Awareness peaks before repeat behavior is built | 90-day ramp with second-visit, reviews, referral and cohort tracking |

These failures share one cause: confusing **uniformity** with **system design**. The better system standardizes data, assets, rules and measurement while adapting the customer-facing choice to local context.

## 16. A 90-day plan for a new franchise CMO or multi-location leader

### Days 1–30: establish truth and trust

**Listen.** Interview the CEO, finance, operations, legal, technology, field leaders, franchise advisory council, high/median/struggling operators, multi-unit and single-unit owners, and recent openers. Ask what the fund promises, what local teams cannot execute, where leads are lost, and which reports nobody believes. IFA’s survey finding that 85% of executives describe franchisee relationships as positive should not substitute for direct operator evidence ([IFA/FRANdata](https://www.franchise.org/2025/02/2025-ifa-franchisor-survey-shows-strong-signs-for-growth-amid-continued-economic-constraints/)).

**Audit.** Reconcile contractual obligations, FDD language, fund receipts/spend, company contributions, co-op balances, vendor contracts, data ownership, agency incentives and active campaigns. Inventory every location, owner, profile, page, phone, CRM/POS/booking connection, media account and review source. Sample at least 10% of units and every unusual format.

**Baseline.** Create a location-level fact table and KPI dictionary. Establish same-store cohorts, brand demand, local visibility, listing accuracy, review rate/SLA, lead response, CAC, CRM identity rate, loyalty behavior, local spend and training adoption. SOCi’s Consumer Behavior Index found 80% of surveyed consumers search for local businesses at least weekly, making broken discovery an urgent early risk ([SOCi Consumer Behavior Index](https://www.soci.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nurturing-the-Customer-Relationship_-Top-Takeaways-from-SOCis-2024-Consumer-Behavior-Index_Superside_Final-compressed-1.pdf)).

**Stabilize immediate hazards.** Correct wrong hours and URLs; secure admin ownership; pause noncompliant claims; fix lead routing; respond to critical reviews; remove expired offers; resolve duplicate campaigns. Issue a plain-language “what is changing / not changing” note.

**Deliverables by day 30:** marketing charter draft, fund map, martech/data map, location master, baseline dashboard, risk register, franchisee interview synthesis and three fast fixes.

### Days 31–60: design the operating system

**Set decision rights.** Ratify the national/regional/local matrix, fixed-flexible-forbidden creative model, vendor policy, approval SLA, crisis escalation, review SLA, data access and franchisee council role.

**Choose priorities.** Score opportunities by revenue potential, customer harm, system reach, speed, confidence and dependency. Usually the first wave is location data, review response, lead routing, opening discipline and a small set of paid plays—not a rebrand.

**Build standards.** Publish the brand-book minimum, claims library, location-page specification, canonical-data schema, campaign taxonomy, measurement dictionary and scorecard. Define comparable cohorts and the test calendar.

**Prototype with operators.** Select 20–40 varied locations: different markets, tenure, performance and ownership scale. Co-design one discovery fix, one reputation workflow, two local paid plays and one CRM journey. Train users and observe them completing tasks.

**Fund transparently.** Present prior spend by objective and the proposed allocation. Explain working versus non-working cost and what will stop. Establish a quarterly fund report and experiment reserve.

**Deliverables by day 60:** approved charter, standards, pilot design, fund dashboard, training modules, vendor scorecard, campaign calendar and measurement plan.

### Days 61–90: prove, learn and scale carefully

**Launch the pilot.** Use matched locations or staggered rollout. Predefine primary outcomes, guardrails, minimum duration and stop rules. Ensure tags, POS/CRM matching and local pages work before spend.

**Create the management cadence.** Weekly pilot review; biweekly franchisee office hour; monthly location scorecard; quarterly fund/business review. Publish help-desk and approval SLA performance.

**Package the repeatable system.** Turn winning tests into one-click plays with audience, asset, budget, landing, training and measurement attached. Document failed tests and context. Announce a rollout sequence based on readiness, not politics.

**Plan the next two quarters.** Balance national brand investment, local conversion, data/loyalty, openings and development. Establish MMM requirements and a geo-experiment roadmap. Yext’s current consumer research shows local discovery is fragmenting into AI as well as traditional search, so the roadmap should monitor new surfaces while keeping authoritative location facts as the foundation ([Yext Consumer Search Behaviors](https://www.yext.com/resources/consumer-search-behaviors-findings)).

**Deliverables by day 90:** pilot readout, scaled playbook v1, location readiness tiers, six-month investment plan, experiment backlog, executive/franchisee scorecard, staffing plan and documented stop-doing list.

### The day-90 standard

Success is not a new campaign. It is that every participant can answer five questions consistently:

1. Which outcomes is marketing accountable for?
2. Which decisions belong to corporate, regional groups and local operators?
3. Where did fund money go, and what evidence governs the next allocation?
4. Which local actions are mandatory, and how quickly are exceptions resolved?
5. Which test will produce the next reliable learning?

When those answers are visible, the network can combine the scale of one brand with the relevance of hundreds of local businesses. That—rather than maximum central control or maximum franchisee freedom—is the durable marketing advantage.
