# Enterprise B2B SaaS Marketing Playbook

## Executive summary

Enterprise SaaS marketing is not scaled-up lead generation. It is the design of market memory, account selection, buying-group consensus, and commercial proof for purchases with roughly $25,000 to $1 million-plus ACV, three-to-18-month cycles, and several constituencies that can stop a deal. Gartner’s current research is more useful than the often-repeated shorthand of an “11-person committee”: buying groups range from five to 16 people across as many as four functions, 74% exhibit unhealthy conflict, and groups that achieve consensus are 2.5 times more likely to report a high-quality deal ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process)). Google and Bain found an average of 17 stakeholders influencing B2B decisions and about 40 touchpoints per buyer, illustrating how a large strategic deal can create hundreds of opportunities for influence ([Google/Bain study in The Wall Street Journal](https://partners.wsj.com/google/how-to-navigate-the-new-b2b-path-to-purchase/)). Treat 11 or 17 as planning signals, not universal constants; instrument the actual buying groups in the company’s CRM.

The operating thesis is simple:

1. **Create future demand broadly enough to be remembered.** The Ehrenberg-Bass/LinkedIn B2B Institute “95-5 rule” argues that most category buyers are out of market at a given time; advertising exclusively to today’s 5% sacrifices future cash flow ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule)).
2. **Capture active demand precisely.** Make the company easy to find in search, review sites, analyst research, communities, and the channels buyers prefer. Gartner describes buying as looping across jobs rather than progressing neatly through a funnel ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)).
3. **Turn account interest into group consensus.** Market to a buying group, not a single “lead.” Gartner reports that buying-group-relevant content improves consensus while individually relevant content can hurt it ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process)).
4. **Measure commercial movement, not marketing activity.** The scorecard is qualified pipeline, opportunity progression, win rate, ACV, sales-cycle velocity, expansion, and efficiency—supplemented by self-reported attribution and incrementality, not replaced by clicks and MQLs.

This is the sales-led and account-based companion to a separate PLG playbook. Product-led signals may enrich account selection, but the center of gravity here is coordinated marketing for committee purchases, seller-assisted evaluation, procurement, and expansion.

## 1. Why enterprise SaaS marketing is different

### The unit of demand is a buying group

A person may download a guide; an organization signs the contract. The relevant object in the database is therefore **account × buying group × buying job**, not a lead. Gartner’s six jobs—problem identification, solution exploration, requirements building, supplier selection, validation, and consensus creation—are revisited nonlinearly ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)). Forrester similarly recommends buying-group-centered lifecycle programs rather than person-based handoffs ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/b2b-marketing/six-steps-to-buying-group-success/)).

Different stakeholders optimize different risks. The economic buyer asks whether the change is worth funding; the champion asks whether it will succeed and improve their standing; end users ask whether it works; IT and security ask whether it is safe and supportable; procurement asks whether terms, price, and vendor risk are acceptable. Marketing must create a shared business case while giving each role the proof needed to endorse it.

Gartner’s survey of 632 buyers found groups of five to 16 across up to four functions and 74% unhealthy conflict ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process)). Google/Bain’s 17-stakeholder finding applies to influence across the path, not necessarily 17 formal voters ([Google/Bain](https://partners.wsj.com/google/how-to-navigate-the-new-b2b-path-to-purchase/)). The implication is not “make 17 personas.” It is to identify roles, shared concerns, vetoes, and the internal communication needed for agreement.

### Buyers are self-directed and reassurance-seeking

Gartner reports that 75% of buyers prefer a rep-free experience, yet fully self-service digital purchases are more likely to produce regret; digital tools used with a rep are associated with 1.8 times the likelihood of a high-quality deal ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)). A later Gartner survey found 61% prefer an overall rep-free experience, reinforcing preference for control without eliminating contextual human help ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)). Marketing should make substantive information freely available and create tools sellers can use collaboratively: diagnostics, TCO models, implementation plans, security packs, and consensus decks.

McKinsey’s B2B Pulse work describes a ten-channel reality, with buyers moving among in-person, remote, and digital self-service interactions ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-new-b2b-growth-equation)). Channel consistency is therefore strategic. A prospect should encounter the same category point of view, proof, and claims on LinkedIn, an analyst page, the website, a dinner invitation, and a seller’s follow-up.

### The market is mostly future buyers

Popularized in B2B practice by the B2B Institute’s Peter Weinberg and Jon Lombardo and grounded in Ehrenberg-Bass buying-behavior research, the 95-5 rule is a corrective to quarterly tunnel vision: roughly 95% of potential B2B buyers are not in market now, so marketing must build memory retrievable when a buying situation emerges ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule)). The Institute’s broader growth principles emphasize reaching category buyers and building mental and physical availability ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/marketing-as-growth)). Category-entry points—situations that cue a category need—bridge brand and demand ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/cep-in-b2b)).

The result is a two-speed system: sustained category-relevant reach for future demand, and high-intensity account activation for current demand. Budgeting only for lead capture makes the brand invisible before a project begins; budgeting only for fame leaves active buyers unsupported.

### Sales and marketing are one buyer experience

Alignment means shared accounts, buying-group definitions, qualification rules, stage evidence, narratives, and retrospectives. Forrester argues that alignment must anchor on the customer and revenue process, not departmental compromise ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/press-newsroom/customer-obsessed-growth-engine-alignment/)). Winning by Design frames revenue as a designed system rather than isolated heroics ([Winning by Design](https://winningbydesign.com/revenue-architecture/)). Marketing owns who to influence, which beliefs to change, what proof to provide, and how to detect movement—even when sales owns the live opportunity.

## 2. Step 1 — Define the ICP and tier accounts

### Build an ICP from evidence, not aspiration

The ideal customer profile is a company-level hypothesis about where the product creates unusual value and where the vendor can repeatedly win. Analyze closed-won, retained, expanded, lost, and failed-implementation accounts: firmographics, technographics, triggers, use case, value realized, deployment friction, cycle, ACV, gross margin, and net revenue retention. Do not confuse a TAM filter (“companies with 1,000+ employees”) with an ICP.

Use a three-part score:

| Dimension | What it measures | Example inputs | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fit** | Structural likelihood of value and successful delivery | Industry, size, geography, architecture, compliance, operating model, use case | Fit is relatively stable; review quarterly |
| **Intent** | Evidence that the account is investigating the problem/category | First-party visits, product activity, review-site research, Bombora topic surge, search | Intent is probabilistic, not a purchase order |
| **Engagement** | Known interaction with the company and buying group | Executive event, reply, meeting, content depth, multiple engaged roles | Weight depth and role coverage above volume |

Bombora describes Company Surge as increased content consumption against a company’s historical baseline, useful for prioritization rather than deterministic buyer identification ([Bombora](https://bombora.com/intent/)). Its guidance applies intent to selection, advertising, and prioritization ([Bombora](https://bombora.com/resources/how-to-use-intent-data/)). G2 and TrustRadius signals can indicate category research nearer evaluation, but only inside their observable universes; silence is not absence of demand ([G2](https://research.g2.com/2024-buyer-behavior-report), [TrustRadius](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/2024-b2b-buying-disconnect-the-year-of-the-brand-crisis/)).

### Tier by economics and ability to personalize

| Tier | Typical scope | Model | Selection logic | Minimum treatment |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: 1:1** | 10–50 accounts per pod | Bespoke | Exceptional fit, material LTV, strategic logo, credible timing | Account thesis, stakeholder map, tailored POV, executive air cover |
| **Tier 2: 1:few** | 50–500 accounts in clusters | Segment plays | Shared use case/trigger and meaningful economics | Cluster narrative, vertical proof, field program, coordinated activation |
| **Tier 3: 1:many** | Hundreds to thousands | Scaled ABM | ICP fit with automated prioritization | Category creation, role content, intent-informed media and routing |

Momentum ITSMA emphasizes client-centricity rather than a campaign veneer ([Momentum ITSMA](https://momentumitsma.com/insights/abm-as-the-catalyst-for-client-centricity)). Tier 1 is constrained by research and orchestration capacity. If every account is Tier 1, none is.

Operationalize it: define positive and negative ICP criteria; score fit first; add triggers such as leadership change, regulation, M&A, cloud migration, or renewal window; aggregate engagement to account and buying group; review acceptance with sales weekly; and re-tier monthly. The output is a named-account portfolio with owners, rationale, expected value, next buying job, missing stakeholders, and assigned program—not a spreadsheet emailed to sales.

## 3. Step 2 — Set category and positioning strategy

### Position from the buyer’s frame

April Dunford’s sequence begins with competitive alternatives: what would customers do if the product did not exist? Then isolate differentiated capabilities, translate them into value, identify who cares most, and choose the market frame that makes value obvious ([April Dunford](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)). The real alternative may be a spreadsheet, internal process, adjacent suite, services, or doing nothing—not merely a named rival ([April Dunford](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/positioning-and-competition)).

A practical canvas includes competitive alternatives; unique attributes; operational, financial, strategic, and personal value; best-fit customers; market category; and proof. Test it against lost-deal notes and unscripted customer language. A statement that sounds distinctive internally but does not alter buyer comparison criteria is copy, not positioning.

### Choose expansion before creation by default

**Category expansion** attaches to an existing budget and process, then differentiates on a wedge. **Category creation** reframes the problem, names a new approach, and seeks to become its reference point. Creation can generate pricing power, but requires years of education, ecosystem proof, analyst work, and patience.

Create a category only when existing categories systematically misdescribe value, a consequential problem is widespread but poorly named, the company can fund sustained education, and customers/partners/analysts will repeat the frame. Otherwise, enter an established category and own a segment, outcome, or category-entry point. LinkedIn’s category-entry-point work supports linking the brand to recurring buying situations ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/cep-in-b2b)). Its B2B Effectiveness Code ties growth to creative and media commitment, warning against underfunded, short-lived programs ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/the-b2b-effectiveness-code)).

Produce a two-page category doctrine: old way, changed world, stakes, new way, category definition, differentiated mechanism, ideal customer, evidence, and excluded claims. It becomes the source for the homepage, analyst briefing, executive talks, campaigns, and seller narrative.

## 4. Step 3 — Build messaging for committee buyers

Use a **problem → solution → value → proof** messaging house:

| Layer | Question answered | Enterprise standard |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Why change? Why now? | Quantify cost/risk of status quo; connect to executive priority |
| Solution | Why this approach? | Explain the mechanism and trade-offs without feature dumping |
| Value | What changes? | Show financial and operational impact, time-to-value, assumptions |
| Proof | Why this vendor? | Peer outcomes, technical proof, plan, third-party validation |

Bain’s B2B Elements of Value spans table stakes, functional outcomes, ease of doing business, individual value, and inspirational value ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/the-b2b-elements-of-value-hbr/)). Messaging should not stop at “save time.” It can reduce risk, simplify integration, strengthen reputation, help a champion’s career, and advance purpose—when evidence supports the claim.

Translate one story by role:

| Role | Primary question | Emphasis | Proof asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Is this worth funding? | Strategic outcome, risk, payback | Executive brief, ROI/TCO, peer reference |
| Champion | Can this win internally? | Urgency, coalition, implementation, political safety | Business-case deck, stakeholder kit |
| End user | Will it improve the work? | Workflow, usability, adoption | Demo, sandbox, user story |
| Technical/security | Is it safe and operable? | Architecture, controls, integrations, governance | Security portal, workshop, DPA |
| Procurement/legal | Is value defensible and risk bounded? | Terms, viability, commercial logic | TCO, compliance and legal pack |

Do not create five unrelated stories. Preserve one narrative and vary the resolution. Gartner found individual-level relevance can harm group consensus, while group-relevant content helps it ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process)). Include bridge language that lets security explain risk to finance and the champion explain adoption to executives.

Every serious opportunity needs buyer-shareable materials: one-page decision brief, current-state diagnostic, stakeholder FAQ, implementation timeline, security packet, business-case model, reference menu, and neutral comparison checklist. Gartner defines buyer enablement as information or tools that make critical buying jobs easier ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/marketing/insights/articles/the-marketers-checklist-to-get-buyer-enablement-content-right)) and recommends balancing supplier-centric content with tools that help complete the purchase itself ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/marketing/insights/articles/rebalance-b2b-content-marketing-for-buyer-enablement)).

## 5. Step 4 — Separate demand creation from demand capture

Chris Walker and Refine Labs popularized a useful distinction: **demand creation** educates buyers not actively searching; **demand capture** converts buyers already seeking a solution. Refine Labs argues for reaching buyers where they learn rather than optimizing everything around forms ([Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/partnerships-0)). Its critique targets gated content, premature SDR follow-up, and attribution-led channel choices that degrade buyer experience ([Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/marketing-tactics-to-change)).

Demand creation includes category POV, executive content, research, podcasts, community, customer stories, and paid distribution. Demand capture includes category and competitor search, review sites, comparison pages, retargeting, demo paths, and high-intent ABM. Plan each asset against a primary job even if it supports both.

A buyer may learn from a podcast, private community, colleague, forwarded PDF, or dinner, then type the brand into search. Analytics credits “direct” or branded search, not causal influence. Refine Labs calls the gap an attribution mirage ([Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/attribution-mirage)); Passetto recommends combining objective approaches instead of treating platform attribution as truth ([Passetto](https://passetto.com/blogs/objective-attribution-measurement-in-marketing)). Add open-text self-reported attribution to high-intent forms and compare it with CRM history, account engagement, analytics, and closed-won interviews.

A content download is not a buying event. Forrester argues that MQL-based processes fragment buying groups and preserve a handoff model misaligned with organizational buying ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/saying-goodbye-to-mqls-whats-the-big-deal-about-getting-rid-of-mqls/)). Replace the central MQL SLA with qualified accounts, verified fit and group engagement, credible triggers, a shared acceptance workflow, feedback reasons, and pipeline outcomes by cohort. Retain person data for consent and personalization, but do not let it define the commercial unit.

## 6. Step 5 — Run ABM as an operating model

ABM is a portfolio design, not a software category. Demandbase’s benchmark work treats maturity as strategy, process, orchestration, data, and measurement—not account-targeted ads alone ([Demandbase](https://www.demandbase.com/resources/report/2024-abm-benchmark/)). Terminus defines ABM around identifying, engaging, and measuring target accounts across the lifecycle ([Terminus](https://terminus.com/account-based-marketing-overview/)).

**1:1 ABM** starts with account insight and a credible change thesis. Build a custom POV, map stakeholders, recruit executive sponsors, and coordinate field, digital, content, partners, and sellers. **1:few** groups accounts by a shared business condition—regional banks facing a regulation, for example—not merely industry. **1:many** scales category and role relevance across a qualified universe, varying investment by signals.

Every play needs eight elements: named audience and exclusions; trigger and buying job; required belief shift; an offer such as research, workshop, peer access, assessment, or evaluation support; coordinated channels; financial, technical, customer, and third-party proof; a progression signal; and a stop/recycle rule. A logo on a landing page is not personalization; an account-specific hypothesis tied to public priorities is.

6sense reports that buyers often complete most evaluation before seller contact and frequently contact a preferred vendor first ([6sense](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/2024-buyer-experience-report/)). The implication is to influence preference before the hand raise, not intensify spam after it. Intent can prioritize, but scoring must be calibrated to actual wins ([6sense](https://6sense.com/blog/dont-call-us-well-call-you-what-research-says-about-when-b2b-buyers-reach-out-to-sellers/)). First-party behavior is most interpretable; review-site research adds category context; third-party topic surge is broad. Require fit plus corroboration before expensive activation.

### ABM governance

- **Weekly account stand-up:** progression, new signals, missing roles, next orchestration.
- **Monthly portfolio review:** tier migration, spend, reach, buying-group engagement, opportunities.
- **Quarterly play review:** conversion lift versus holdout or matched comparison, deal feedback, content gaps.
- **Biannual ICP review:** retention, expansion, win/loss, implementation success.

Validate intent quarterly: among high-intent accounts, what share entered qualified pipeline relative to matched low-intent accounts? Bombora explicitly frames surge against an account’s own research baseline ([Bombora](https://bombora.com/intent/)); G2 reports show discovery increasingly includes peers and AI-mediated research ([G2](https://learn.g2.com/2025-g2-buyer-behavior-report)). ABM fails when marketing and sales use different lists, personalization is cosmetic, or impressions masquerade as commercial movement.

## 7. Step 6 — Build POV-led content and thought leadership

Enterprise thought leadership should teach buyers how the world changed, reveal a hidden cost or opportunity, and offer a defensible way to act. The 2024 Edelman–LinkedIn report found strong thought leadership can make decision-makers more receptive and help lesser-known vendors compete ([Edelman and LinkedIn](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2024-b2b-thought-leadership-report)). The 2025 report centers “hidden buyers”—stakeholders in finance, operations, legal, procurement, and compliance who consume ideas and can block a deal despite limited seller contact ([Edelman and LinkedIn](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report)).

A strong thesis has tension: a widely held belief is wrong or incomplete; evidence explains why; the new model has practical implications. Commission original research when proprietary data or access can illuminate the issue. Pair a flagship report with executive essays, short videos, charts, webinars, field discussions, and seller-ready excerpts. Long form is the intellectual source; distribution is the system.

| Buying job | Buyer question | High-value content |
|---|---|---|
| Problem identification | Is this important enough to change? | Benchmark, diagnostic, cost-of-inaction model, executive POV |
| Solution exploration | What approaches exist? | Category guide, architecture patterns, neutral options analysis |
| Requirements building | What must it do? | Checklist, RFP guide, security and integration standards |
| Supplier selection | Who can deliver? | Comparative proof, demo, customer evidence, implementation plan |
| Validation | Can the case survive scrutiny? | ROI/TCO, references, risk pack, pilot design |
| Consensus | Can stakeholders align? | Decision brief, stakeholder FAQ, workshop, mutual plan |

Gartner recommends content that helps customers complete buying tasks, not just understand the vendor ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/marketing/insights/articles/rebalance-b2b-content-marketing-for-buyer-enablement)). Content Marketing Institute research identifies strategy, scalable operations, and measurement as differentiators of effective B2B programs ([Content Marketing Institute](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/b2b-research/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research-2025)). Demand Gen Report’s preferences research supports personalized, data-backed, shareable material that helps teams advance decisions ([Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/resources/2023-content-preferences-survey-report-personalized-data-backed-content-enables-b2b-teams-to-fast-track-buyer-enablement-speed-up-path-to-purchase/7815/)).

Maintain one canonical brief per asset: audience, buying job, belief change, evidence, SME, core story, formats, distribution, sales use, measurement, and expiration. Interview practitioners and customers; do not outsource original thinking to a generic writer. Create modular evidence blocks—charts, claims, quotes, cases, objections—so teams adapt without inventing facts. Tag by segment, role, buying job, use case, proof type, region, and freshness.

Measure account reach, quality consumption, return visits, sharing, buying-group penetration, progression, seller adoption, and qualitative mention in calls. Downloads are distribution data, not demand.

## 8. Step 7 — Use field marketing, events, and executive programs

Events create trust, access, and group progress that digital channels cannot. Use a portfolio:

- **Executive dinners:** 8–16 peers around one urgent issue; no product pitch; executive host and curated seating.
- **Roundtables:** facilitated problem-solving with a practitioner or analyst; publish anonymized insights.
- **Workshops:** account-specific diagnostic or business-case work tied to a buying job.
- **User conferences:** customer learning, roadmap, community identity, advocacy, and expansion.
- **Trade shows:** category presence, partner meetings, executive access, and pre-booked target-account conversations—not badge volume.
- **Regional micro-events:** efficient 1:few activation for account clusters.

LinkedIn’s event guidance connects engagement to pipeline impact rather than registrant totals ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/trends-tips/b2b-event-marketing-pipeline-impact)). McKinsey’s omnichannel research supports a mix of in-person, remote, and self-service experiences ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/b2b-sales-omnichannel-everywhere-every-time)).

For each event, name target accounts, roles, commercial hypothesis, seller follow-up, and 30/60/90-day progression measures. Give sales an attendee brief with account context and conversation prompts. Within 48 hours, send useful synthesis. Route explicit requests immediately; place non-buyers into relevant education without calling them MQLs.

At sales kickoff, train the market narrative, account plays, proof, and content use—not the campaign calendar. At QBRs, report account coverage, buying-group gaps, opportunity progression, field impact, and next-quarter plays. Marketing earns credibility when field programs create executive relationships and move named opportunities.

## 9. Step 8 — Build analyst and influencer relations

Gartner vendor briefings let vendors present strategy, product, and differentiation; they are not paid placement ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/contact/vendor-briefings)). Inquiries reveal market definitions, client questions, and evaluation criteria. The Magic Quadrant evaluates Ability to Execute and Completeness of Vision under a published methodology ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/research/methodologies/magic-quadrants-research)). The Forrester Wave uses a transparent evaluation process for a defined market ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/policies/forrester-wave-methodology/)); IDC MarketScape assesses supplier capabilities and strategies ([IDC](https://www.idc.com/promo/idcmarketscape/)).

Run a 12-month calendar: priority analysts, coverage, inquiries, briefings, research windows, product milestones, references, and spokespeople. Brief with a crisp category view, honest roadmap, differentiation, customer evidence, and traction. Track feedback and close the loop. Never imply that participation guarantees placement.

Influence is an ecosystem. Practitioners, community leaders, consultants, partners, customer MVPs, newsletter writers, and technical creators may shape shortlists more directly than broad-reach personalities. Map them by audience trust and buying-stage relevance. Offer experts, data, customers, and product access without buying praise; establish disclosure and editorial-independence rules.

Community MVP programs work when recognition follows contribution. Provide early education, peer access, speaking opportunities, and a product-team channel. Measure credible content, community answers, referrals, references, and account influence—not follower totals.

## 10. Step 9 — Execute digital across paid, SEO, and review sites

### Paid media

LinkedIn is often the dominant paid social channel for enterprise SaaS because of company, role, seniority, and matched-audience targeting—not because every click is cheap. LinkedIn describes a large professional audience of decision-makers and influencers ([LinkedIn Marketing Solutions](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/linkedin-ads/linkedin-b2b-audience)). Dreamdata’s benchmarks focus on revenue contribution and return on ad spend, more useful than CTR alone ([Dreamdata](https://dreamdata.io/linkedin-ads-b2b-benchmarks)). Metadata’s analysis of $42 million in Facebook and LinkedIn spend shows results vary by audience, objective, and creative; external averages are priors, not targets ([Metadata](https://metadata.io/benchmark/what-we-learned-from-42m-in-spend-on-facebook-and-linkedin)).

Use four program types: broad ICP reach with distinctive POV; tier/cluster activation tied to a trigger; category/competitor search and other demand capture; and sequenced retargeting with proof and buyer enablement. Suppress customers, employees, converted accounts, and exhausted audiences. Avoid over-frequency to tiny matched lists. Rotate messages, not just colors. Use account/geographic holdouts where scale permits.

### SEO and branded truth

Cover category-entry points, problem language, category terms, use cases, integrations, industries, alternatives, comparisons, implementation, security, pricing approach, and branded queries. Google’s B2B research emphasizes digital research across the path, often before direct contact ([Google](https://business.google.com/us/think/future-of-marketing/b2b-marketing-strategies/)). Build expert pages that answer questions fully, not thin keyword permutations.

Maintain branded search hygiene: accurate titles and sitelinks; monitor “reviews,” “pricing,” “security,” “alternatives,” and “[brand] vs.”; keep analyst and customer proof findable; correct outdated profiles. For AI-mediated answers, make claims, entities, authorship, evidence, and structured facts easy to verify.

### Review sites

G2, TrustRadius, and Gartner Peer Insights appear during shortlist and validation. G2 documents evolving discovery and shorter shortlists ([G2](https://research.g2.com/2024-buyer-behavior-report)). TrustRadius emphasizes review quality and buyer trust over volume ([TrustRadius](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/building-buyer-trust-review-quality-report-2025/)). Gartner publishes formal listing and review-management guidance ([Gartner Peer Insights](https://gpivendorresources.gartner.com/en/articles/8212705-getting-listed-on-peer-insights)).

Request reviews after real value moments; invite a representative customer set; never script sentiment. Respond to criticism specifically. Mine language for positioning, objections, and product gaps. Measure profile views, intent signals, shortlist presence, influenced opportunities, and reference-quality evidence.

## 11. Step 10 — Build sales enablement and content operations

Enablement is the last mile of marketing strategy. Highspot’s research emphasizes unified content, training, coaching, and analytics ([Highspot](https://www.highspot.com/resource/state-of-sales-enablement-report-2025/)); Seismic recommends tracking adoption and business outcomes, not repository activity alone ([Seismic](https://www.seismic.com/resources/guides/guide-critical-enablement-kpis/)). A platform supports governance but cannot fix weak content.

Minimum system:

- **Core narrative deck:** modular, role-aware, evidence-backed.
- **Discovery/POV guides:** problem hypotheses, questions, category story.
- **Battle cards:** alternative, when it wins, traps, proof, discovery questions, response—not insults.
- **Proof library:** cases indexed by industry, use case, size, role, outcome, and reference status.
- **ROI/TCO calculator:** transparent assumptions, editable ranges, sensitivity analysis, finance-readable output.
- **Security/procurement hub:** architecture, controls, certifications, DPA, insurance, accessibility, viability.
- **Mutual decision kit:** timeline, roles, evidence, milestones, buyer-owned actions.
- **Deal-desk content:** packaging rationale, value summary, concession exchange, implementation options.

Every asset needs an owner, review date, version, buying job, and measurement. Archive aggressively; search should return one trusted answer, not seven decks.

Review wins and losses monthly: which claim opened the deal, objection stalled it, alternative mattered, proof was missing, and stakeholder appeared late? Feed learning into positioning, campaigns, analyst relations, and product marketing. Highspot’s buyer-enablement guidance reinforces helping buyers navigate decisions, not merely helping sellers pitch ([Highspot](https://www.highspot.com/blog/buyer-enablement/)).

## 12. Step 11 — Turn customers into advocacy and expansion

Customer marketing begins at handoff. Set advocacy expectations during onboarding, but request participation only after verified value. Segment customers by outcome, relationship health, strategic fit, story strength, and preferred contribution.

Use an advocacy ladder: private feedback/advisory council; review or community answer; reference call; quote or case study; webinar or event panel; then strategic co-marketing and executive advocacy. Do not equate NPS with willingness or permission. NPS is a signal; confirm outcome, relationship, and consent. Maintain a reference database with use limits so the same champions are not exhausted. Forrester treats reference management and community as connected advocacy capabilities ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/report/Effective-Customer-Reference-Management-Anchors-B2B-Community-Marketing-Efforts/RES54578)).

### Make land-and-expand a marketing motion

Map whitespace by team, geography, use case, product, and executive priority. Package realized value into business reviews, internal success stories, peer sessions, adoption campaigns, and executive outcome reports. Product telemetry may reveal an opportunity, but expansion messaging must connect usage to business value. Reforge argues that enterprise expansion requires product and go-to-market systems to work together ([Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/enterprise-growth)); Kyle Poyar’s Growth Unhinged coverage shows how packaging and usage models shape expansion economics ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-state-of-usage-based-pricing)).

Customer marketing KPIs include priority-feature adoption, multi-threaded relationships, reference coverage, review quality, community participation, expansion pipeline, net revenue retention influence, and churn-risk engagement. Marketing supports the outcome; it should not claim sole causality.

## 13. Step 12 — Measure the system

### Use a metric hierarchy

| Level | Metrics | Decision enabled |
|---|---|---|
| Business | New ARR, expansion ARR, NRR, CAC payback, gross margin | Is the model creating durable value? |
| Pipeline | Qualified pipeline, coverage, sourced/influenced pipeline, win rate, ACV, velocity | Is marketing improving throughput? |
| Account | ICP reach, engaged accounts, buying-group penetration, stage progression, intent lift | Are priority accounts and roles moving? |
| Program | Cost per qualified opportunity, event progression, search capture, content use, incremental lift | Which programs deserve investment? |
| Diagnostic | Reach, frequency, completion, CTR, conversion, form rate | Why did the program perform as it did? |

Pipeline coverage should be calculated by segment and period, not one blended number. Required pipeline equals target bookings divided by expected win rate, adjusted for timing and slippage. Velocity can be represented as opportunities × win rate × ACV divided by cycle days, but inspect each component because the composite hides deterioration. Winning by Design encourages modeling the full recurring-revenue system rather than isolated stages ([Winning by Design](https://winningbydesign.com/revenue-architecture/)).

Define attribution terms in writing:

- **Marketing-sourced:** marketing generated the first verified opportunity-creating interaction under an agreed rule.
- **Marketing-influenced:** a meaningful marketing interaction occurred among buying-group members in a defined window; report depth, not an inflated binary total.
- **Self-reported:** the buyer names the source; useful for dark social, subject to recall bias.
- **Multi-touch attribution:** allocates observable credit; useful operationally, not causal.
- **Marketing mix modeling:** estimates aggregate contribution from time-series variation; useful at sufficient scale.
- **Incrementality:** holdout or quasi-experimental estimate of what happened because of the program; strongest causal evidence when feasible.

Dreamdata aggregates account-level touches across long B2B journeys ([Dreamdata](https://docs.dreamdata.io/article/oncuus42fm-journey-metrics)). HubSpot documents several attribution models, illustrating that model choice changes credit rather than revealing one truth ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/attribution-reports-definition)). Refine Labs recommends hybrid measurement combining software attribution, self-report, and other evidence ([Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/hybrid-attribution-framework)).

### Executive dashboard

Report monthly by ICP segment and account tier: qualified pipeline created and influenced; coverage against future bookings; cost per qualified opportunity and CAC trend; stage conversion, age, cycle, and win rate; ACV and discount movement; buying-group role coverage; top programs with incremental or triangulated evidence; expansion pipeline and advocacy coverage; and three insights, decisions, and risks.

Do not present 40 channel metrics to the board. Maintain diagnostic dashboards for operators and a commercial narrative for executives. Dreamdata’s GTM benchmarks can provide external context, but the company’s cohort history should be the primary baseline ([Dreamdata](https://dreamdata.io/blog/b2b-go-to-market-benchmarks-2024)). Metadata benchmarks can flag outliers in media execution but should never become revenue targets ([Metadata](https://metadata.io/b2b-advertising-benchmarks)).

## 14. Common anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| **MQL worship** | Person activity is disconnected from group intent | Qualified accounts, buying-group coverage, opportunity progression ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-revenue-process-alignment-series-part-1-the-end-of-mqls/)) |
| **Gating everything** | Trades trust and reach for low-intent contacts | Ungate education; gate only when fulfillment or declared intent creates value |
| **Last-touch truth** | Credits capture for demand created elsewhere | Triangulate multi-touch, self-report, interviews, MMM, incrementality ([Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/attribution-mirage)) |
| **ABM as a tool purchase** | Produces targeted impressions without insight or sales coordination | Portfolio, tiers, plays, governance, outcomes ([Demandbase](https://www.demandbase.com/resources/report/abm-maturity-report/)) |
| **Ignoring dark social** | Underfunds where trust and memory form | Self-report, buyer interviews, branded-search trends, cohort evidence |
| **Persona proliferation** | Fragments the narrative and creates content debt | One narrative, role translations, consensus assets |
| **Intent as certainty** | Triggers aggressive outreach on noise | Fit-first scoring, corroboration, decay, validation ([Bombora](https://bombora.com/resources/how-to-use-intent-data/)) |
| **Personalization theater** | Logos and tokens add no relevance | Account insight, trigger, hypothesis, and proof |
| **Content without distribution** | High production cost, low memory | Paid, executive, partner, community, seller, SEO, and field distribution |
| **Events measured by badges** | Rewards crowds rather than progress | Target-account roles, meetings, stage movement, 90-day pipeline |
| **Analyst relations as PR** | Misses learning and category feedback | Year-round inquiries, briefings, evidence, roadmap loop |
| **Case-study vanity** | Stories lack buyer credibility | Baseline, intervention, time frame, outcome, caveats, customer voice |
| **One global CAC** | Hides segment and cycle economics | Cohort CAC/payback by segment, motion, geography, tier |
| **Quarterly brand switch** | Prevents memory among future buyers | Sustained reach linked to category-entry points ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule)) |
| **Content dumping ground** | Sellers cannot find or trust material | Taxonomy, single source, expiry, enablement, outcome feedback |

## 15. A 90-day plan for a Series A–C enterprise SaaS marketing leader

The first 90 days should produce a validated commercial model and a few working plays—not a rebrand, martech migration, and 40-campaign calendar at once. First Round Review’s go-to-market library consistently emphasizes learning from customers and sequencing company-building decisions rather than copying a generic playbook ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/articles/go-to-market/)). a16z describes a modern enterprise motion in which product signals, growth, and sales can complement one another, but the design must match ACV and buying complexity ([a16z](https://a16z.com/growthsales-the-new-era-of-enterprise-go-to-market/)). Lenny’s enterprise playbook similarly highlights the distinct process required for large contracts and multiple stakeholders ([Lenny’s Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-enterprise-sales-playbook-1m-to-10m-arr)). SaaStr’s enterprise guidance reinforces that moving upmarket changes the buying and enablement system, not merely contract size ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/running-the-enterprise-playbook-with-envoy-head-of-enterprise-sales-sarah-lash-video/)). HubSpot’s State of Marketing research is useful as a broad channel and practice scan, but company-specific pipeline cohorts should govern allocation ([HubSpot Research](https://www.hubspot.com/state-of-marketing)).

### Days 1–30: diagnose and align

**Customer and market**
- Interview 10–15 customers across new wins, expansions, and at-risk accounts; add at least five losses or stalled opportunities.
- Reconstruct three journeys from first memory to signature, including hidden roles, channels, objections, and proof.
- Audit category, alternatives, review sites, analyst coverage, branded search, and customer language.
- Read product usage, onboarding, retention, expansion, and support patterns to distinguish attractive logos from successful customers.

**Revenue system**
- Segment pipeline by ACV, industry, size, source, use case, stage, age, win rate, and cycle.
- Define opportunity, stage entry/exit, sourced/influenced, ICP, intent, engagement, and tier with sales and revenue operations.
- Inspect the top 25 open deals: stakeholder map, next buying job, content gaps, technical/procurement risk, executive coverage.
- Audit MQL routing, consent, campaign taxonomy, attribution, paid waste, and content usage.

**Outputs**
- Baseline scorecard and funnel/account model.
- Draft ICP, exclusions, buying-group map, and message gaps.
- Stop/start/continue list and named-account pilot.
- Two urgent fixes, often branded-search hygiene and a security/procurement hub.

**Day-30 gate:** leadership agrees on priority segment, economics, qualification, pipeline target, and no more than three market beliefs to change.

### Days 31–60: design the system

**Positioning and narrative**
- Run a positioning workshop using alternatives, differentiated attributes, value, best-fit buyers, category, and proof ([April Dunford](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)).
- Produce category doctrine, messaging house, role translations, claims/evidence register, and objection map.
- Validate with five customers, five sellers, and representative prospects; revise based on comprehension and comparison behavior.

**Portfolio and programs**
- Finalize fit + intent + engagement scoring and Tier 1/2/3 rules.
- Select 10–20 Tier 1 pilot accounts and one coherent 1:few cluster; assign sales owners.
- Design one demand-creation platform around a category-entry point and one demand-capture sprint across search, review sites, comparison pages, and conversion paths.
- Create the editorial thesis, quarterly flagship, executive distribution plan, and content repurposing workflow.
- Build the consensus kit: decision brief, ROI model, security index, implementation outline, reference menu, and comparison guide.

**Operations**
- Establish weekly account stand-up, monthly pipeline review, shared dashboard, campaign naming, and closed-loop acceptance reasons.
- Set SLAs for explicit demo requests and qualified account signals without forcing every content consumer to sales.
- Define privacy, consent, and data-retention rules for first- and third-party signals.

**Day-60 gate:** each priority program has an owner, target-account list, buying-job hypothesis, offer, channel plan, seller action, progression measure, and stop rule.

### Days 61–90: launch, learn, and reallocate

**Launch**
- Start the 1:1 pilot with account theses, executive outreach, tailored proof, and one high-value workshop offer.
- Launch the 1:few play with segment POV, paid reach, seller sequence, and a roundtable or peer program.
- Publish and distribute the first POV asset; keep core education ungated.
- Repair high-intent capture: category search, branded pages, review profiles, demo routing, retargeting, and self-reported attribution.
- Train sales on narrative, proof, battle cards, and consensus kit; certify through application, not attendance.

**Measure and learn**
- Track account reach, engaged roles, explicit interest, meetings, opportunity creation, stage progress, win/loss signals, and cost per qualified opportunity.
- Interview responders and non-responders; listen to calls; inspect how sellers use materials.
- Compare activated accounts with a matched or holdout group where feasible.
- Reallocate from weak audience/message combinations; do not kill future-demand work because it lacks immediate last-touch pipeline.

**Day-90 deliverables**
1. Approved ICP and tiered account portfolio.
2. Evidence-tested positioning, messaging house, and category doctrine.
3. One operating 1:1 play and one repeatable 1:few play.
4. Demand-creation and demand-capture portfolio with explicit jobs and budgets.
5. Buyer-enablement and sales-enablement minimum system.
6. Analyst, review, customer advocacy, and executive-event roadmaps.
7. Measurement dictionary, dashboard, attribution triangulation, and experiment backlog.
8. Six-month hiring and investment plan based on bottlenecks, not org-chart convention.

### Suggested weekly operating cadence after day 90

| Cadence | Meeting | Decisions |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Account/pipeline stand-up | Promote/recycle accounts, fill stakeholder gaps, assign next action |
| Biweekly | Editorial and distribution review | Approve POV, evidence, formats, and channel plan |
| Monthly | Revenue review | Reallocate budget by pipeline quality, progression, and learning |
| Monthly | Customer evidence council | Select advocates, cases, reviews, analyst references, expansion stories |
| Quarterly | Strategy/QBR | Revisit segment economics, positioning evidence, portfolio, and capacity |
| Semiannual | ICP and category review | Adjust fit model, category-entry points, narrative, and long-term investment |

## Appendix A — Operating templates

### Account plan: the one-page marketing view

For every Tier 1 account—and for the highest-value Tier 2 opportunities—maintain a living one-page view that marketing, the account executive, sales development, customer success, and the executive sponsor can read in minutes. It should contain:

1. **Account thesis:** why this account should care now, expressed as a falsifiable business hypothesis rather than generic firmographics.
2. **Commercial potential:** expected initial ACV, plausible expansion path, strategic value, implementation feasibility, and major disqualifiers.
3. **Trigger evidence:** leadership changes, declared initiatives, technology migration, regulatory deadline, hiring, M&A, operational incident, renewal, or first-party behavior.
4. **Buying group:** named or hypothesized economic buyer, champion, users, technical/security roles, procurement/legal, and influential outsiders.
5. **Current buying job:** the most likely Gartner job the group is trying to complete; this prevents marketing from sending supplier-selection content during problem identification ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)).
6. **Belief barriers:** what the group must believe about the problem, approach, vendor, implementation, and economics before it can proceed.
7. **Proof available and missing:** relevant customer, quantified outcome, technical architecture, security evidence, analyst view, review, partner validation, and executive reference.
8. **Orchestration plan:** coordinated marketing and seller actions for the next 30 days, with an owner and intended progression signal.
9. **Consent and sensitivity notes:** contact preferences, restricted topics, procurement rules, and relationship context.
10. **Exit criteria:** evidence that justifies continued investment, promotion, recycling, or removal.

The account plan is not a surveillance dossier. Use lawful business data, minimize personal data, document provenance, and favor useful relevance over invasive personalization. Third-party intent should influence portfolio prioritization, not create claims that a named individual performed an anonymous action.

### Campaign brief: one page before production

Every enterprise campaign should answer the same questions before creative or media work begins:

- **Business objective:** which pipeline, expansion, category, or retention outcome matters?
- **Audience:** which account tier, segment, roles, and exclusions?
- **Buyer context:** which trigger and buying job make the program timely?
- **Single-minded belief:** what should the audience believe after exposure?
- **Evidence:** which data, customers, expert sources, product capabilities, and caveats support the claim?
- **Offer and experience:** what useful thing can the buyer consume or do without meeting sales?
- **Distribution:** how will paid, owned, earned, partner, field, executive, community, and seller channels reinforce one another?
- **Sales action:** what should the seller do, with whom, and only after which signal?
- **Measurement:** leading diagnostic, account progression, pipeline outcome, comparison group, time horizon, and decision threshold.
- **Reuse:** what modules should become seller content, event material, SEO pages, analyst proof, and customer communication?

The brief forces a choice. A campaign cannot simultaneously target every segment, move every belief, and optimize brand reach, form fills, opportunities, and closed revenue. Name the primary objective and use secondary measures diagnostically.

### Proof hierarchy and evidence quality

Not all proof is equal. Order claims by credibility and proximity to the buyer’s context:

| Proof type | Strength | Required context |
|---|---|---|
| Audited or customer-verified economic outcome | Very high | Baseline, time period, scope, attribution, caveats |
| Comparable customer reference | Very high | Similar industry, use case, scale, architecture, and role |
| Technical validation or controlled pilot | High | Success criteria, environment, data quality, limits |
| Independent analyst or verified review pattern | Medium–high | Market scope, methodology, recency, sample quality |
| Vendor benchmark from aggregated data | Medium | Population, exclusions, calculation, representativeness |
| Product claim or feature demo | Medium | Exact capability, configuration, dependencies, roadmap status |
| Testimonial without specifics | Low | Identity, role, and experience; do not present as outcome proof |

The goal is not to eliminate vendor claims; it is to make the path from claim to evidence visible. Maintain a claims register with wording approved for public use, source, methodology, owner, expiration, geographic limits, and customer permission. Review high-risk security, financial, comparative, and forward-looking claims with the appropriate internal specialists.

## Appendix B — Budget and team design

A Series A–C enterprise marketing budget should follow constraints and growth thesis, not a universal percentage. Allocate across enduring capabilities and flexible programs:

- **Market foundation:** research, positioning, brand system, website, analyst relations, communications, and customer evidence.
- **Demand creation:** thought leadership, creative, paid reach, executive programs, community, flagship field moments, and partnerships.
- **Demand capture:** paid search, SEO, review sites, conversion paths, retargeting, and high-intent account activation.
- **Buyer and sales enablement:** product marketing, proof, ROI tools, security/procurement content, enablement platform, and training.
- **Customer marketing:** adoption communications, community, advocacy, user events, and expansion programs.
- **Data and operations:** CRM/automation, account model, enrichment, measurement, experimentation, and privacy controls.

Protect a test budget and a durable brand/category allocation from weekly pipeline panic. Conversely, do not label all unmeasured activity “brand.” Every investment needs a strategic hypothesis, appropriate leading evidence, and a time horizon. LinkedIn’s B2B Institute argues that effective B2B growth requires both broad reach and commitment over time ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/the-b2b-effectiveness-code)); the 95-5 rule explains why immediate conversion cannot be the only evaluation window ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule)).

Build the team around bottlenecks. Early on, a strong product marketer often has outsized leverage because positioning, buyer research, sales enablement, launches, and proof are usually underdeveloped. Demand leadership must span creation and capture rather than inherit an MQL quota. Field marketing becomes critical when account concentration and executive access justify it. Customer marketing should arrive before references and expansion become emergencies. Marketing operations should be an early strategic capability, not late-stage dashboard support.

Agency use should add scarce expertise or elastic capacity—research design, creative platform, paid operations, production, analyst preparation—not replace internal market understanding. Keep positioning, customer insight, editorial conviction, claims governance, and cross-functional decisions close to the company.

## Appendix C — Decision rules for the leadership team

Use explicit rules to protect strategy from anecdote:

- **Add an account to Tier 1** only when fit, potential value, a credible account insight, and delivery capacity are all present.
- **Trigger seller outreach** only after declared intent or a corroborated account signal; content consumption alone enters nurture or research.
- **Gate content** only when the exchange itself creates value, such as a personalized diagnostic, live workshop, benchmark output, or requested follow-up.
- **Launch a category** only when the existing frame obscures value and the company can sustain education for years.
- **Sponsor a trade show** only with target-account density, pre-booked meetings, partner/analyst objectives, a distinctive experience, and a follow-up owner.
- **Commission a case study** only when the result is verified, relevant, specific, permissioned, and not already overrepresented in the proof library.
- **Buy an ABM platform** only after the target-account process, data ownership, sales workflow, plays, and measurement are defined.
- **Cut a channel** when repeated, well-executed tests show poor incremental account or pipeline movement—not merely because last-touch credit is low.
- **Scale a program** after message-audience fit and operational repeatability are demonstrated; scaling before either multiplies waste.
- **Refresh positioning** when win/loss, category, product, or customer evidence changes—not because internal stakeholders are bored.

These rules create consistency across quarterly pressure. They also make disagreement productive: teams can challenge the evidence or threshold rather than debate taste.

## Closing principle

Enterprise marketing’s job is not to manufacture leads for sales. It is to make the company memorable before a project exists, discoverable when one begins, credible when alternatives are compared, useful while a committee decides, and valuable enough after purchase to expand and advocate. The 95-5 rule explains why future demand matters ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule)); Gartner’s buying-group research explains why consensus matters ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process)); McKinsey’s omnichannel work explains why the experience must cohere across channels ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-new-b2b-growth-equation)). The durable system balances all three and measures the commercial movement that follows.
