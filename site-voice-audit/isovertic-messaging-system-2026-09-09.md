## Ground rules for every page

1. **Write for the reader in their title.** Chief scientific officer, chief medical officer, medical director, principal investigator, practice administrator, hospital marketing lead, vice president of commercial, director of billing, chief executive officer, president, founder. Address them as they refer to themselves.
2. **No marketing jargon in client-facing copy.** Do not use any of these terms in copy a client will read: `funnel`, `top of funnel`, `bottom of funnel`, `MQL`, `SQL`, `PQL`, `MOFU`, `BOFU`, `ICP`, `TAM`, `CAC` (spell out cost to acquire a customer), `LTV` (spell out lifetime value), `attribution model`, `buyer journey`, `ABM`, `PLG`, `growth loop`, or `content flywheel`.
3. **No developer or SEO jargon.** Do not use server-rendered, static site generator, Next.js, hub and spoke, schema, structured data markup, taxonomy, canonical, headless, JAMstack, indexation, crawl budget, or SERP.
4. **Do not describe our method for choosing what to publish, what to bid on, or what to build.** No mention of long-tail keywords, one page per term, per-condition or per-code page architecture, keyword clustering, entity SEO, seven layers, or any specific technique for how we decide the site's contents. Say we have a method and what it produces. Not how.
5. **Describe deliverables in words the reader would use in a status meeting.** A monthly report. A published article. A newsletter that goes to your list. Eight social posts. A landing page for the campaign. A qualified meeting on your calendar with a written brief. A cited page a specialist can send to their compliance officer.
6. **Every load-bearing number carries a real citation with a real URL.** Copy the URL exactly.
7. **Small dry warmth is allowed. Stand-up comedy is not.** Warmth in the industry pages, directness in the method pages.

## Part 1. The one sentence and the reframe

### The one sentence

**We build the website that gets found by the specialist already searching for what you sell, then we spend advertising only on those people.**

That sentence is the anchor. Every page traces back to it.

### The reframe every buyer has to make

Founders who raised capital learned marketing as creating belief in something that did not yet exist. It is how a Series A deck works. That habit makes them read a website as a brochure, and marketing as spending money to get people to notice.

The site is not a brochure. The site is your best salesperson, working alone, at 3 a.m., when a chief scientific officer or a director of billing types a specific question into a search bar. If the site answers the question in language the specialist recognizes, they book a meeting with your team. That is the whole trick.

The rest of the offer (paid search, media, outbound, training) exists to feed that motion or extend its reach. It is not a substitute for it.

### Why this works for our clients

Our clients sell to people who search in exact language. A gene name. A billing code. A regulation. A device model. A condition. A protocol. Those searches are not crowded, and they are being made every day by the exact people you want in a meeting.

**We do not explain further than that in public.** Our method for choosing what to publish, what to bid on, and what to build is the work you are paying for. Any page that spells it out is a page a competitor can copy.

### Proof, before mechanism

Lead with numbers, cite the source, then stop.

- **Ingenious Targeting Laboratory:** monthly form submissions moved from 13 to about 64. Organic search traffic rose 3.7 times. First eight months. Internal, from HubSpot and Google Search Console, September 2026. Public case-study rule: multiples and percentages only unless the client has approved raw counts in writing.
- **Sydra Health:** company had no online presence in mid-June 2026. Now roughly one qualified meeting per week on about $1,000 monthly ad spend plus $2,500 monthly site work. Internal, September 2026.

### The two honest limits

Every proposal, every homepage, every industry page carries these. They are what make the claim credible.

1. **This works when your buyers already search in specific language.** If nobody is searching for your category yet, this method cannot find them. We will tell you before you pay.
2. **A qualified meeting a week on a small budget is a trickle, not a flood.** That can still be the right result for the spend. We do not promise a flood.

### Words we do not use to describe ourselves

Creating demand. Brand awareness. Funnel. Growth hacking. ROI framework. Full-service agency. Full-funnel agency. Marketing partner. Content flywheel. Growth loop. Category creation.

Those describe another playbook or another kind of shop. They are fine in a client's own campaign when they apply. They are not how we describe us.

## Part 3. The six steps, in plain language

Every engagement uses the same six steps. The package sets which steps we run and how deeply. What we do NOT publish in public: the method for choosing what to publish, what to bid on, or what to build. That is the work.

### 01 Build, a website that answers the questions your buyers actually type

**What it does.** Your website is the salesperson who works the room when nobody is available. Build gives it the pages it needs to answer the specific questions your buyers ask, so it can hand a qualified meeting to your team. It is the foundation of every other step.

**What you get.**
- A full website rebuild, included in every package ($15,000 value across the year)
- Hosting included
- You own your code and your domain
- A site that loads fast and can be found by search engines and AI answer engines
- A site that presents proof, pricing, and the next step clearly on every page

**Honest limit.** We build on our own stack. If you must stay on Wix or Squarespace, this is not the right shop for you and we will say so.

### 02 Rank, content that gets your company found and cited

**What it does.** A steady stream of articles, newsletters, and social posts that answer the questions your buyers ask before they book a meeting. Written by people who understand your work, reviewed against your evidence and claim rules, and published on cadence.

**What you get each month (varies by package).**
- Published articles on your site (one at Ground State, more at higher packages)
- A newsletter that goes to your list
- Eight to 16 social posts across your active channels
- At Amplification and above: a YouTube channel with research, script, production, and publishing
- A short monthly report on what was published, how it performed, and what is next

**Honest limit.** Search results compound. You will start to see it from month three and it accelerates from month six. If you need meetings faster than that, pair Rank with paid.

### 03 Run, Google Ads measured in booked meetings

**What it does.** Google Ads built and managed against the specific searches your buyers actually make. The metric that matters is a qualified meeting on your calendar and what it cost to book. Not clicks, not impressions.

**What you get.**
- One campaign at Excitation, more at Amplification and above
- One landing page per campaign, refreshed as needed
- Weekly build, review, and adjustment
- A monthly report with cost per booked meeting

**Honest limit.** Below about $1,000 a month in Google Ads spend, the platform cannot learn enough to produce reliable results. If that is your budget, Ground State plus organic is the honest recommendation.

### 04 Buy, streaming television, broadcast, and radio when the math works

**What it does.** Media buying across streaming (CTV), broadcast, and radio for growing companies that would once have been priced out. Creative, buying, and measurement from one team, tied to meetings on your calendar.

**What you get.**
- Available on Amplification and Quantum Leap
- Creative, buying, and reporting from one team
- Attribution that ties spend back to inbound activity

**Honest limit.** Media is worth it when name recognition helps the sale (regional healthcare, hospitality, high-consideration business-to-business). It is not the right first move for a company still testing whether search engines can even find them.

### 05 Book, qualified meetings on your calendar, with a written brief

**What it does.** The full outbound motion, run by our team. A list built from real data sources, sequences written by the same people who write your website, phone and email and LinkedIn, qualification against your ideal client, and meetings booked on your calendar with a written brief for each one.

**What you get.**
- One outbound seat at Amplification with a written commitment for the number of meetings per month
- Two seats at Quantum Leap
- Add a seat to any package at $4,500 / month
- Conference sprints at $7,500 per event

**Honest limit.** Outbound before your website can defend the pitch is a waste of your buyer's time. If your site does not confirm what the email claims, we sequence the Build first, then start dialing.

### 06 Educate, Protocol training so your team can run the system

**What it does.** We hand over the runbooks. One or two days at your office, eight runbooks written in your brand, and a follow-up call at 30 days. The Google Ads runbook is written by the Google Ads specialists. The compliance runbook is written by our compliance officer. The life-science runbook is written by our molecular biologist. Nothing is generic.

**What you get.**
- One department, one day, $5,000. Up to 15 people, one step of the system in depth.
- Full team, two days, $20,000. Up to 40 people, all six steps plus a developer track.
- Add-on for current clients, $3,500. Same day, run for your team.

**Honest limit.** This works when your team can commit one focused day. If they cannot, do not buy it.

### Where Brand and Design fits

Brand and Design is not a seventh step. It is a supporting layer that runs across all six steps: identity, message system, sales materials, and interface design. It is included in Amplification and Quantum Leap, and available as an add-on to lower packages. The current /services page numbers Brand as step 06 and Educate as 07. ## Part 4. Voice, split by page type

Both voices are the Isovertic voice. What changes is which register leads.

### Method-forward voice, for pages that explain what we do

**Pages.** /, /system, /protocol, /services, /pricing, /audit, /seo-content, /google-ads, /tv-ctv-advertising, /web-development, /outbound-appointment-setting, /design-brand.

**Rules.**
- Open with the honest big thing (the numbers or the outcome), not reader empathy.
- Short declarative sentences.
- Every claim carries a footnote, a real URL, or a named case.
- Reader is a founder, chief executive officer, chief scientific officer, chief medical officer, or vice president of commercial. They want the specification.
- Warmth is expressed as directness and honest limits, not as reassurance.
- CTA: send us your [artifact], in about ten minutes we will tell you [specific answer].

### Client-facing warmth, for pages where the reader is under pressure

**Pages.** /industries/*, /problems/*, /field-notes/*, /answers/*, /long-island, /contact, /compliance/*.

**Rules.**
- Open by acknowledging the reader is smart and something is off. Confirm the anxiety, then promise the payoff.
- Reader is a practice administrator, medical director, principal investigator, director of billing, chief scientific officer, compliance officer, or operations lead.
- Contractions everywhere. First and second person.
- Small dry warmth that lands.
- Cite everything load-bearing with a real URL.
- CTA: send us your [artifact], in about ten minutes we will tell you [specific answer]. Sometimes the honest answer is you are already fine, and we are happy to say so and hand you back your afternoon.

### The banned list, both voices

- No em-dashes, no en-dashes in prose. Numeric ranges as "6 to 12," not `6–12`.
- No unicode ellipses. Three periods or rewrite.
- No banned AI-tell phrases. See `references/ai-tells.md` for the full list. The audit script catches every one.
- No marketing acronyms in client copy. Spell out.
- No developer or SEO jargon in client copy. Spell out or replace.
- No description of the method for choosing what to publish, what to bid on, or what to build. Say we have a method and what it produces.

Run the audit script on every page before it ships.

## Part 5. Objection responses (memorize these)

**Why is the minimum a year?**
Because a year is the shortest window in which pipeline and revenue can move on a long sales cycle. Google's own guidance says search improvements take four months to a year, per [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names). Healthcare sales average 125 days to 24 months. A shorter term would have you judging revenue before a deal could close, and 43% of business-to-business agency churn happens in the first 90 days for exactly that reason, per the [Focus Digital 2026 churn report](https://focusdigital.co/agency-churn-benchmarks-2026/).

**Why should I pay $2,500 a month when a freelancer would do it for $800?**
Because a freelancer produces content. We produce a pipeline. The Ground State package includes a $15,000 website rebuild across the year, a full content program on cadence, and one team accountable for the number at the end. Compare what shows up each month.

**Why not 90 days first, then commit?**
Because 90 days is the exact window in which a long sales cycle shows no revenue yet, which is exactly when 43% of clients quit, which is exactly why we will not take a 90-day deal. If a shorter term is a requirement, we will refer you to a shop that does 90-day pilots and wish you well.

**We already have a marketing person.**
Good. Protocol trains them. Or Ground State runs the content around them so they can spend their time on strategy. Or, if they are running the whole pipeline and it is working, we are the wrong shop and we prefer to say so.

**Do you use AI to write the content?**
We use AI in research and tools. Every published page is planned, edited, and fact-checked by a person, and reads like it. If a page reads like AI slop, it does not ship. The audit script catches the tells before the page goes live.

**Can we cancel?**
Yes. The unbilled balance of the $15,000 website build is due on the final invoice, prorated to months served. Nothing else. Your code and your domain stay with you.
