## Keep the Work In House, With a Process

You may already have a marketing person, a sales hire, a compliance officer, and a developer. Good. Protocol does not replace them. It gives them a documented way to work together across Build, Rank, Run, Buy, Book, and Educate. Your team learns what each step is for, what gets produced, who approves it, and what to measure. The runbooks are written in your name so they can be used after we leave.

## The Six Steps Your Team Learns

**Build.** The website that answers buyer questions and clearly presents proof, pricing, and the next step. Your team gets the rules for reviewing a page before it goes live and the definition of a page that is ready for a chief medical officer or a director of billing.

**Rank.** The cadence of published articles, newsletters sent to your list, and social posts. Your team gets a publishing routine and a review process that keeps evidence close to the claim.

**Run.** Google Ads managed against qualified meetings and cost per meeting. Your team learns how to review the account and the landing page without judging the work by clicks alone.

**Buy.** Streaming television, broadcast, and radio when recognition helps the sale. Your team learns the approval and reporting process, including when it is not worth buying media.

**Book.** Outbound that produces qualified meetings on the calendar with written briefs. Your team learns the meeting definition, the handoff to sales, and the follow-up rules.

**Educate.** The runbooks, the meeting cadence, and the owner for each part of the system. This is the step that makes the other five stick after we go home.

We do not publish the internal method for deciding what gets built, published, or purchased. Your Protocol day teaches your team how to operate the system without giving competitors a public instruction manual.
